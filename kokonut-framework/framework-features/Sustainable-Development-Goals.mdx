This page maps the Kokonut Framework's community-owned syntropic farm model to all 17 United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. It is designed for grant writers, institutional partners, and impact investors conducting SDG due diligence on Kokonut Network farms.

**Two tiers of SDG contribution:**

- **Primary SDGs (1, 2, 5, 8, 15):** Directly addressed by every Kokonut Framework farm, with specific, verifiable metrics tracked through the [MRV stack](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification) and anchored as on-chain [EAS attestations](https://docs.attest.sh/). These are the SDGs for which Kokonut can produce audited, cryptographically verifiable evidence — not self-reported claims.
- **Secondary SDGs (3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17):** Indirectly or potentially contributed to by Kokonut farms, depending on farm location, scale, and operational maturity. These contributions are real but not yet the subject of the same rigorous on-chain verification as the primary five. **See the primary SDGs in action at [Adelphi →](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/sustainable-development-goals)**

The Adelphi SDG page documents SDGs 1, 2, 5, 8, and 15 with specific metrics, capital contributions, measurement methodology, and EAS attestation UIDs — the evidence base for every primary SDG claim in this Framework-level mapping.

All capital contributions below reference the [8 Forms of Capital](/kokonut-framework/framework-components/framework-features/8-forms-of-capital): Natural, Financial, Social, Human, Material, Intellectual, Cultural, and Health.

---

## Community-Owned Farms, Forms of Capital, and SDGs Alignment

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="No Poverty">
    The syntropic farm model addresses poverty not just through job creation, but by building community resilience. By diversifying local food production and creating value-added products, it reduces dependency on external economic factors. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Financial Capital:** The farm provides direct employment and income opportunities for community members — at Adelphi, 7 full-time positions and ~\$149,110 in projected annual gross revenue distributed within the local Monte Plata community
    - **Social Capital:** The community ownership model ensures a broader distribution of benefits — the DAO governance structure and 10% public goods allocation route value back to the community rather than extracting it upward

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Contributes to SDG 8 (**Decent Work and Economic Growth**) by providing sustainable livelihoods
    - Supports SDG 2 (**Zero Hunger**) by improving local food security
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Zero Hunger">
    Syntropic farming's focus on biodiversity and perennial crops creates a robust, resilient food system less susceptible to climate shocks or pest outbreaks. The integration of trees and crops (agroforestry) provides diverse nutritional options year-round. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Diverse, resilient food production system — at Adelphi, three simultaneous crop cycle lengths (short vegetables, medium fruit, long coconut) ensure continuous food production regardless of any single crop's seasonal variability
    - **Cultural Capital:** Preservation of local food traditions and native crop varieties through the endangered species nursery — 12\+ at-risk Dominican species propagated and distributed free to neighboring communities

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) through climate-resilient agriculture
    - Contributes to SDG 15 (**Life on Land**) by promoting biodiversity
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Good Health and Well-being">
    The farm's organic practices ensure chemical-free, nutritious food, directly impacting physical health. The community engagement aspect provides social connections and purpose, and the connection to living land and nature supports mental well-being. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Production of diverse, organic foods — at Adelphi, complete elimination of synthetic pesticides and fertilizers means zero chemical residues in any produce; 100 eggs per day from free-range hens provide locally sourced protein access
    - **Cultural Capital:** Connection to nature and community through the farm's educational programs, weekend community activities, and the restorative experience of working with living soil and growing things

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 11 (**Sustainable Cities and Communities**) by providing green spaces and community gathering facilities
    - Contributes to SDG 12 (**Responsible Consumption and Production**) through organic farming practices
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Quality Education">
    The farm serves as a living laboratory for sustainable agriculture education. It partners with local schools and community organizations for field programs, apprenticeships, and intergenerational knowledge transfer — particularly important in communities where traditional farming knowledge is at risk of being lost. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Intellectual Capital:** Development and sharing of sustainable farming knowledge — at Kokonut, this is institutionalized through open-source documentation ([wasalo/kokonut-framework](https://github.com/wasalo/kokonut-framework)) and the Mintlify docs site as a freely accessible knowledge-sharing platform
    - **Human Capital:** Hands-on learning opportunities in agriculture and ecology — at Adelphi, the multipurpose education gazebo hosts regular workshops on organic farming, biochar production, syntropic planting, and agroforestry for community members of all ages

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 9 (**Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure**) through agricultural innovation and open-source methodology development
    - Contributes to SDG 8 (**Decent Work and Economic Growth**) by building skills and employability in regenerative agriculture
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Gender Equality">
    The community ownership model provides a platform to ensure gender equality in decision-making, profit-sharing, and leadership roles. Kokonut farms implement policies that actively promote women's participation across all farm operations — from fieldwork to governance. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Social Capital:** Inclusive community ownership and decision-making processes — at Adelphi, founded and operated by sisters Yanny and Neury Hernández, women hold all ownership, operational management, financial, and community program leadership roles
    - **Human Capital:** Equal opportunities for skill development and leadership roles — the farm's training programs are designed to build capabilities across genders, and the women-led model demonstrates agricultural leadership pathways for other women in the region

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 10 (**Reduced Inequalities**) by promoting inclusive economic participation
    - Contributes to SDG 8 (**Decent Work and Economic Growth**) by tapping into the full productive potential of the community workforce
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Clean Water & Sanitation">
    Syntropic farming practices — no-till methods, diverse plant cover, and year-round cover crops — significantly improve soil structure and water retention. This reduces runoff and erosion, naturally filtering water and recharging local aquifers. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Improved water retention and filtration through healthy soil ecosystems — at Adelphi, beard grass ground cover on all terraced edges prevents topsoil erosion and runoff; syntropic root systems maintain water pathways through dry periods; elimination of synthetic inputs removes the primary source of agricultural chemical contamination of waterways
    - **Material Capital:** Development of sustainable water management through soil organic matter building rather than energy-intensive infrastructure

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) by improving resilience to droughts and floods
    - Contributes to SDG 15 (**Life on Land**) by preserving soil and water ecosystems
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Affordable and Clean Energy">
    Syntropic farming dramatically reduces on-farm energy consumption by eliminating mechanical tillage and synthetic input supply chains — the two most energy-intensive activities in conventional agriculture. The closed-loop biological fertility system (biochar, humic acids, organic urea — all produced on-site) has zero embedded energy cost after establishment. Future Kokonut farm phases may integrate renewable energy systems such as solar panels or small-scale biogas digesters using farm waste. This is a planned pathway, not a current deployment at Adelphi — transparency about this distinction is important for SDG reporting integrity.

    **Capital Contributions**

    - **Material Capital:** Potential for on-site renewable energy production in future farm phases; current energy reduction through elimination of tillage and synthetic input dependency
    - **Intellectual Capital:** Development of energy-efficient farming practices — the syntropic methodology is documented as open-source, enabling other farms to replicate the energy reduction profile

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 9 (**Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure**) through clean energy innovation and documentation
    - Contributes to SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) by reducing current reliance on fossil fuel-embedded synthetic inputs
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Decent Work and Economic Growth">
    The syntropic farm model promotes economic growth that is sustainable and inclusive. It provides diverse job opportunities beyond traditional farming roles — including in processing, marketing, education, community programming, and ecological monitoring. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Financial Capital:** Sustainable business model providing local employment — at Adelphi, 7 full-time positions with diverse skill requirements; ~\$149,110 projected annual gross revenue retained within the Monte Plata local economy
    - **Human Capital:** Skill development in sustainable agriculture and related fields — the Framework's four development phases build operational capability progressively; the education gazebo provides ongoing skills training for community members beyond the farm's direct workforce

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 1 (**No Poverty**) by providing sustainable livelihoods
    - Contributes to SDG 10 (**Reduced Inequalities**) through inclusive economic participation
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure">
    The syntropic farm serves as a hub for agricultural innovation, developing and testing sustainable farming methods that can be replicated and improved across the network. The Kokonut Agentic Marketplace represents the infrastructure innovation layer — AI agents automating MRV reporting, harvest forecasting, and impact scoring. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Intellectual Capital:** Development of innovative agroforestry and sustainable farming techniques — systematized in the open-source [Kokonut Framework](https://github.com/wasalo/kokonut-framework) and continuously improved through the Technology Guild and Agentic Marketplace
    - **Material Capital:** Creation of sustainable agricultural infrastructure — the Farm Registry API, Data Hub, and EAS attestation pipeline constitute a verifiable, open-access agricultural data infrastructure

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 12 (**Responsible Consumption and Production**) through sustainable agricultural innovations
    - Contributes to SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) by developing climate-smart, data-verified agricultural practices
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Reduced Inequalities">
    The community ownership structure of the farm provides a model for reducing economic inequalities. DAO governance and contribution-weighted Guild Points create mechanisms for profit-sharing, democratic decision-making, and economic participation for community members regardless of their initial capital position. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Social Capital:** Inclusive community ownership model — the [Kokonut Guilds DAO](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/kokonut-guilds-dao) enables anyone who contributes work to earn Guild Points and eventually Loot tokens, providing economic stake without requiring capital to tribute
    - **Financial Capital:** Equitable distribution of farm benefits — 10% public goods allocation routes revenue to community activities; the DAO rage-quit mechanism ensures no minority holder's capital can be trapped against their will

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 1 (**No Poverty**) by providing economic opportunities for disadvantaged groups
    - Contributes to SDG 5 (**Gender Equality**) through inclusive participation and women-led leadership
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Sustainable Cities and Communities">
    While Kokonut's current farms are rural — Adelphi is located in Gonzalo, Monte Plata, not near a major urban center — the Framework is designed to scale to peri-urban contexts where productive landscapes can be incorporated into community design, providing food, recreation, and ecosystem services for nearby urban populations. The community infrastructure at Adelphi — the education gazebo, the weekend programs for children and the elderly, the free native plant distribution — demonstrates how a rural farm can function as a community hub that serves the surrounding area, regardless of urban proximity.

    **Capital Contributions**

    - **Social Capital:** Creation of a community hub and shared resource — the farm serves as the primary gathering and educational space for the batey and Haty community in the Gonzalo region
    - **Natural Capital:** Provision of green space and ecosystem services — the agroforestry structure and endangered species nursery improve the ecological value of the surrounding landscape

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 3 (**Good Health and Well-being**) by providing access to nature and fresh organic food
    - Contributes to SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) by creating rural carbon sinks and watershed protection
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Responsible Consumption and Production">
    The syntropic farm embodies responsible production through its regenerative practices, minimal external inputs after establishment, and zero-waste approach. It promotes responsible consumption by connecting consumers directly with their food source and demonstrating the value of diverse, locally produced crop varieties. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Sustainable, regenerative production methods — the closed-loop system (poultry manure → humic acids → crop beds → forage → poultry) eliminates external waste streams; biochar production from on-farm bamboo converts a boundary management byproduct into a permanent soil amendment
    - **Cultural Capital:** Promotion of sustainable consumption patterns — the farm's market relationships with local organic businesses and supermarkets create direct consumer-to-producer connections that support cultural awareness of food provenance

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) through low-emission, closed-loop production methods
    - Contributes to SDG 15 (**Life on Land**) by demonstrating agriculture that enhances rather than depletes ecosystems
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Climate Action">
    Syntropic farming is inherently climate-positive, sequestering carbon in soil and perennial plant biomass while eliminating the emissions associated with synthetic inputs and mechanical tillage. The diverse, multi-strata system is also more resilient to climate extremes than monoculture alternatives. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Carbon sequestration through agroforestry and soil building — at Adelphi, estimated at 0.4–1.2 metric tons CO₂e per acre per year, with an additional ~18% boost from biochar application; tracked via satellite NDVI and annual soil tests
    - **Intellectual Capital:** Development of climate-resilient farming practices — the Kokonut Framework documents and shares these practices as open-source methodology available to any farm globally

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 2 (**Zero Hunger**) by creating climate-resilient food systems less susceptible to drought and extreme weather
    - Contributes to SDG 15 (**Life on Land**) through practices that enhance natural carbon sinks in soil and woody biomass
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Life Below Water">
    While not directly working with marine ecosystems, the farm's sustainable practices significantly reduce the agricultural runoff that is one of the primary drivers of coastal water degradation. Eliminating synthetic inputs and maintaining soil cover prevents the nutrient loading that causes algal blooms and marine dead zones in downstream coastal areas. The ocean impact methodology documented in Adelphi's [MRV stack](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification) includes seaweed-based organic fertilizers and periodic coastal cleanup activities — connecting farm operations to marine ecosystem health.

    **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Reduction of agricultural runoff and synthetic chemical pollution from Kokonut farms; seaweed collection for fertilizer production removes oceanic accumulation while providing on-farm inputs
    - **Cultural Capital:** Education about the land-sea connection — community programs at Adelphi include environmental awareness about watershed health and the farm's downstream impact on local waterways

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 6 (**Clean Water and Sanitation**) through improved water quality in farm watersheds
    - Contributes to SDG 12 (**Responsible Consumption and Production**) by reducing harmful agricultural inputs that eventually reach marine systems
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Life on Land">
    The syntropic farm actively enhances terrestrial ecosystems, creating habitat for diverse flora and fauna while producing food and income. It demonstrates how agriculture can coexist with and actively support natural ecosystems rather than replacing them. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Natural Capital:** Enhancement of biodiversity and ecosystem services — at Adelphi, 12\+ endangered native species in the on-site nursery, multi-strata agroforestry planting creating wildlife habitat, and satellite-verified vegetation health tracking via NDVI, NDRE, and MSAVI indices
    - **Intellectual Capital:** Knowledge development about sustainable land management in the Dominican Republic's specific ecological context — documented in open-source format and contributed back to the Kokonut Framework for use by future farms

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 13 (**Climate Action**) through enhanced ecosystem resilience and carbon sequestration
    - Contributes to SDG 2 (**Zero Hunger**) by creating biodiverse, resilient food production systems
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions">
    The community ownership and DAO governance structure of Kokonut farms serves as a small-scale, replicable model for participatory governance with transparent decision-making, on-chain accountability, and verifiable benefit-sharing. The DAO governance framework — no admin keys, rage-quit protection, on-chain vote execution — demonstrates institutional design principles that apply beyond farming. **Capital Contributions**

    - **Social Capital:** Development of inclusive, transparent community governance structures — the [Kokonut Governance Framework](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/governance-framework) is publicly documented, auditable on-chain, and designed to be amended by community proposal rather than administrative decree
    - **Cultural Capital:** Fostering a culture of cooperation and shared responsibility — the Manifesto's six Freedom principles (Choice, Work, Power, Access, Pay, Opinion) are the cultural operating system of the network

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports SDG 10 (**Reduced Inequalities**) through inclusive governance where capital and contribution both earn voice
    - Contributes to SDG 5 (**Gender Equality**) by ensuring equal access to governance participation regardless of gender
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Partnerships for the Goals">
    Kokonut Network operates through an ecosystem of partnerships — technical, institutional, financial, and community — that enable the farm network's impact to compound across domains. **Current Kokonut ecosystem partnerships:**

    | **Partner** | **Contribution** | **SDG relevance** |

    **Capital Contributions**

    - **Social Capital:** Building local and regional partnerships with farmers, public goods funders, Web3 infrastructure providers, and ecological impact frameworks — each partnership extends the network's reach and credibility
    - **Intellectual Capital:** Sharing knowledge and best practices through open-source repositories, the Mintlify documentation site, and the [Kokonut Agentic Marketplace](/kokonut-x-ai-agents) as a public infrastructure for farm data automation

    **SDGs Interconnections**

    - Supports all other SDGs by creating collaborative networks for sustainable agricultural development
    - Particularly contributes to SDG 4 (**Quality Education**) and SDG 9 (**Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure**) through knowledge partnerships and open-source methodology
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

---

This deep dive illustrates the profound and interconnected ways in which a community-owned syntropic farm can contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals. By holistically addressing environmental, social, and economic aspects of sustainability, this model offers a microcosm of sustainable development in action.

The synergies between different forms of capital and SDGs demonstrate the potential for cascading positive impacts, where progress in one area naturally leads to advancements in others.

> As we face global challenges like climate change, biodiversity loss, and food insecurity, models like the Kokonut Framework offer practical, scalable solutions that align with and actively contribute to the achievement of the SDGs.
>
> They provide not just a means of sustainable food production, but a blueprint for community resilience, ecological regeneration, and inclusive economic development.
