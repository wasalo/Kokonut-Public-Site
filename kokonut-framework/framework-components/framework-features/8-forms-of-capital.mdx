The 8 Forms of Capital is a regenerative economics framework — developed by Gregory Landua and Ethan Roland in their work on regenerative enterprise — that expands the conventional definition of capital beyond money to include all the forms of value that healthy communities and ecosystems generate and depend on.

Kokonut uses the 8 Forms as the measurement architecture for annual impact reporting: where the [Pillars of Value](/kokonut-framework/framework-components/pillars-of-value) define _what_ to evaluate, the 8 Forms define _how_ to measure value creation across every dimension of farm impact. Together they form the complete impact assessment framework that undergirds every Kokonut farm's annual [EBF report](https://ebfcommons.org/).

The eight forms are assessed annually at every Kokonut farm and published to the [Data Hub](https://hub.kokonut.network). The EBF Framework's four reporting dimensions map directly to these eight capital forms:

| **EBF Dimension** | **Capital forms** |
| --- | --- |
| Environmental Impact | Natural Capital |
| Economic Impact | Financial Capital |
| Social Impact | Social \+ Human Capital |
| Sustainability Audit | All 8 forms |

## Implementation and Measurement Strategies

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Natural">
    **What it is:** The ecological health of land, water, air, biodiversity, and ecosystem services — the living foundation that all other capital forms depend on. **Implementation**

    - Conduct soil and biodiversity baseline assessments before planting — at Kokonut farms this uses the [MRV ground sensing stack](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification): soil moisture probes measuring volumetric water content, electrical conductivity, and soil temperature across crop beds
    - Build soil organic matter through [syntropic farming methodology](/kokonut-framework/why-syntropic-farming): biochar incorporation, year-round cover crops, closed-loop poultry manure cycling, and multi-strata perennial planting — not drip irrigation or synthetic mulch, which create input dependency rather than self-regulating soil health
    - Implement phased planting across all three cycle lengths: at [Adelphi](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/summary), 96 coconut trees across 8 plots (long cycle), 560 passion fruit plants across 8 plots (medium cycle), and lettuce across 10 plots (short cycle) — calibrated to the specific 15,725 m² site

    **Measurement**

    - Annual soil quality tests via ground probes: organic matter accumulation, nutrient availability (EC readings), and microbial activity indicators — all published to the [Data Hub](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41)
    - Quarterly biodiversity surveys tracking changes in local flora and fauna; species count in the endangered species nursery maintained as a living catalog; per-plant health logged via [Silvi](https://www.silvi.earth/) GPS
    - Satellite vegetation monitoring on each Sentinel overpass: NDVI (general health), NDRE (maturity phase), MSAVI (early season) — processed via QGIS and logged to the MRV pipeline
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Financial">
    **What it is:** Monetary resources, revenue streams, investment flows, and the financial mechanisms that enable farms to operate, scale, and distribute value — including the governance structures that determine who controls capital allocation. **Implementation**

    - Fund farms through the [Kokonut DAO](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/dao-layers) treasury mechanism: stablecoin tribute → DAO proposal → on-chain vote → milestone-gated disbursements anchored to EAS-attested MRV data. This replaces grant and microloan dependency with a community-governed, rage-quit-protected capital allocation system
    - Establish transparent financial management through on-chain treasury: all inflows and outflows visible on [Gnosisscan](https://gnosisscan.io/address/0xeb55b75328a8dffd45bbf34b7e7efc431a179085) in real time; no admin can move funds without a passed DAO proposal
    - Allocate **10% of gross revenue** to the public goods fund — at [Adelphi](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/crops-and-harvest-forecast), approximately **\$14,911/yr** at current projections — supporting community workshops, free native seedling distribution, and the education gazebo

    **Measurement**

    - Monthly revenue tracking from crop sales (lettuce, passion fruit, coconut) and poultry production (eggs), published per cycle to the [Data Hub](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41)
    - Quarterly financial reports including gross revenue, public goods distribution amount, and treasury balance — all on-chain and auditable via [DAOHaus](https://link.kokonut.network/dao)
    - Annual impact report detailing public goods fund deployment: number of workshops, seedlings distributed, participants trained, and community programs funded
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Social">
    **What it is:** The relationships, networks, trust, and coordination mechanisms within and around the farm community — the social fabric that enables collective action, shared governance, and community resilience. **Implementation**

    - Build community governance through the [Kokonut Guilds DAO](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/kokonut-guilds-dao): six domain-specific contributor bodies (Technology, Impact, Communications, Governance, Finance, Community & Partnerships) that coordinate farm operations, impact reporting, and ecosystem development through contribution-weighted Guild Points rather than capital-weighted voting
    - Form community advisory relationships with local stakeholders — at Adelphi, Yanny and Neury Hernández actively engage the batey and Haty community near Gonzalo, organizing weekend programs for children, the elderly, and neighboring farmers
    - Develop regular community events: workshops at the education gazebo, weekend recreational and educational programs, and the free distribution of native seedlings that builds ongoing relationships with neighbors

    **Measurement**

    - Quarterly employment reports: number of positions, wage levels, and job satisfaction — logged via Atlantis App and published to the [Data Hub](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41)
    - Annual community surveys assessing project perception, community cohesion, and awareness of the farm's public goods programs
    - Guild Points accumulation tracked per contributor and per Guild — a quantitative measure of social capital building within the Kokonut Network ecosystem; Guild membership and contribution history published via [Charmverse](https://link.kokonut.network/charmverse)
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Human">
    **What it is:** The knowledge, skills, health, and capabilities of the people who operate and benefit from the farm — the human capacity that enables both agricultural excellence and community leadership. **Implementation**

    - Develop training through the [Kokonut Framework's four development phases](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-i): Phase I includes mandatory personnel training in agro-ecological practices, bio-input production, sustainable soil management, and market strategies — customized to the specific farm's crop selection and community context
    - Operate the on-site agro-ecological education center: at Adelphi, a multipurpose gazebo serves as dining area, meeting room, and training facility — hosting regular workshops on organic farming, agroforestry design, biochar production, ecological regeneration, and poultry management
    - Partner with local schools and community organizations to integrate agricultural education into the surrounding community — not just the farm's workforce

    **Measurement**

    - Pre- and post-training assessments to measure skill acquisition per workshop cohort
    - Track number of participants in educational programs by session, topic, and community of origin — logged via Atlantis App field data
    - Annual mentorship and training assessment: skill transfer rates, program satisfaction, and participant follow-on agricultural activity in their own communities
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Material">
    **What it is:** Physical infrastructure, tools, equipment, and built assets that enable farm operations and community benefit — the material foundation of a working agricultural system. **Implementation**

    - Build essential farm infrastructure through DAO-funded capital deployment: at Adelphi, [Public Nouns Proposal #69](https://publicnouns.wtf/vote/69) funded the critical infrastructure — production beds, the on-site biochar production system, the poultry facility for 110 hens, the multipurpose education gazebo, and the endangered species nursery
    - Develop efficient, syntropic-aligned water management: terraced terrain with beard grass ground cover rather than energy-intensive drip irrigation; soil organic matter building as the primary water retention strategy
    - Establish on-site biological input production: the biochar production station (bamboo → pyrolysis → biochar), the poultry manure processing system (manure → humic acids \+ organic urea), and the nursery propagation infrastructure

    **Measurement**

    - Quarterly progress reports on infrastructure: build completion status, maintenance requirements, and operational capacity relative to the farm's development phase
    - Annual assessment of infrastructure condition and expansion needs — tied to the [Framework Phase](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-i) the farm is currently in
    - Track utilization of community facilities: workshop attendance at the education gazebo, nursery propagation output per season, poultry facility egg production records
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Intellectual">
    **What it is:** Knowledge systems, research outputs, documented methodologies, and the shared information infrastructure that allows the network to learn, improve, and replicate — including open-source tools and data. **Implementation**

    - Maintain and improve the open-source Kokonut knowledge infrastructure: [wasalo/Kokonut-Public-Site](https://github.com/wasalo/Kokonut-Public-Site) (this documentation), [wasalo/kokonut-framework](https://github.com/wasalo/kokonut-framework) (Framework spec and methodology), and [wasalo/Kokonut-Agentic-Marketplace](https://github.com/wasalo/Kokonut-Agentic-Marketplace) (onchain agent infrastructure) — all open source and forkable by any community
    - Document and share operational knowledge through the [Farm Registry API](/build-with-kokonut#framework-data-primitives): MRV data, harvest records, and impact metrics accumulated at Adelphi become the training data and reference implementation for every subsequent Kokonut farm
    - Develop the [Kokonut Agentic Marketplace](/kokonut-x-ai-agents) as an intellectual capital multiplier: AI agents that automate MRV reporting, harvest forecasting, and impact scoring accumulate operational knowledge that improves with each farm cycle

    **Measurement**

    - Number of open-source contributions per quarter: pull requests, documentation improvements, schema updates, and agent deployments — tracked via GitHub repository activity
    - Data Hub usage metrics: number of farm records indexed, MRV events attested, and API queries per month — measuring intellectual capital accessibility
    - Knowledge replication rate: how many new farms have successfully used the Framework documentation to independently complete Phase I planning — the ultimate measure of intellectual capital quality
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Cultural">
    **What it is:** The traditions, stories, practices, and values that connect communities to their land, their history, and each other — the cultural continuity that gives regenerative agriculture its deepest meaning. **Implementation**

    - Preserve and celebrate the founders' stories: at Adelphi, [Yanny and Neury Hernández's journey](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/background-story) — from pharmaceutical and manicurist careers in the city to building a regenerative farm on land their grandparents farmed — is documented as a public good, demonstrating that the reconnection to traditional land stewardship is possible and viable
    - Conserve native and heritage crop varieties through the endangered species nursery: 12\+ at-risk native species from the Dominican Republic — including native cacao, guavaberry, Congo coffee tree, Hispaniola palmetto — are propagated and distributed free to neighboring communities, preserving the agrobiological heritage of the region
    - Design community programs that reconnect younger generations to agricultural knowledge: weekend programs at Adelphi invite children from the batey and Haty community to participate in educational, recreational, and farming activities — transmitting knowledge that would otherwise be lost as rural communities urbanize

    **Measurement**

    - Number of heritage variety propagations per season: species count in the nursery, seedlings distributed to community members, and recipients' planting follow-through rates
    - Community program participation: attendance at weekend events, age distribution of participants, and feedback from community members on cultural relevance and value
    - Documentation completeness: the founders' story, traditional farming practices, and indigenous species knowledge archived in the public documentation and Data Hub — permanently accessible to future farm operators
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Health">
    **What it is:** The physical and mental wellbeing of the people in and around the farm — directly supported by access to clean, nutritious food, safe working conditions, and the restorative connection to nature that farming communities provide. **Implementation**

    - Produce organic, chemical-free food as the primary health capital contribution: at Adelphi, the complete elimination of synthetic pesticides and fertilizers means that every vegetable, fruit, and egg produced is free of the chemical residues that persist in conventionally farmed food — directly improving the health of both farm workers and consumers
    - Expand community protein access through the poultry system: 110 free-range hens producing approximately **100 eggs per day** make locally produced protein consistently available to the Monte Plata community — at lower cost and higher nutritional quality than processed or imported alternatives
    - Implement comprehensive occupational health and safety protocols for farm contributors: ergonomic farming practices, training in safe biochar production (pyrolysis involves high heat), and proper protective equipment for poultry management and soil work

    **Measurement**

    - Annual community health metrics: access to fresh organic produce, consumption of locally produced food, and reported reduction in synthetic food additive exposure — tracked via community surveys
    - Farm worker health outcomes: occupational injury rates, illness incidence, and access to occupational health resources — reported annually in the EBF impact report
    - Egg and produce quality testing: periodic nutritional content analysis confirming that Adelphi's organic production delivers measurably superior nutritional profiles compared to conventional alternatives
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## How the 8 Forms map to annual reporting

Every Kokonut farm's annual [EBF Framework](https://ebfcommons.org/) impact report is organized around the 8 Forms of Capital — each form contributing to one or more of the four EBF reporting dimensions:

| **Capital form** | **EBF dimension** | **Primary metrics at Adelphi** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Natural** | Environmental | Carbon sequestration (t CO₂e/acre/yr), NDVI average, biodiversity index, species count |
| **Financial** | Economic |  |
| **Social** | Social | Guild Points accumulated, community programs held, workshop attendance |
| **Human** | Social | Training participants, skills assessments, mentorship pairs |
| **Material** | Sustainability | Infrastructure completion status, facility utilization rates |
| **Intellectual** | Sustainability | Open-source contributions, API usage, knowledge replication rate |
| **Cultural** | Sustainability | Heritage species preserved, community programs, founders' story documented |
| **Health** | Sustainability | Organic food access, worker health incidents, nutritional quality audits |

Annual reports for Adelphi are published to [hub.kokonut.network/projects/41](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41) and anchored as EAS attestations on Gnosis Chain — making the 8 Forms assessment not just claimed, but cryptographically verifiable.
