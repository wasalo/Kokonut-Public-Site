# Phase IV is the continuous evidence and learning function across every phase.

**Phase IV — Continuous Operations** does not wait for Phase III.

It begins when Phase I creates the first usable baseline and continues for as long as the farm, its records, or its claims remain active.

Its purpose is to keep the farm **legible over time**:

- what changed;
- what stayed stable;
- what failed;
- what was repaired;
- what was harvested;
- what was sold;
- what was forecast;
- what became actual;
- what evidence became stale;
- what risks materialized;
- what practices were implemented;
- what outcomes were measured;
- what claims need to be narrowed;
- what decisions should change next.

> **Time does not make evidence stronger by itself. Comparable, well-governed records over time make stronger analysis possible.**

## Phase IV at a glance

| Question | Phase IV answer |
| --- | --- |
| **What is it?** | The continuous evidence, review, maintenance, learning, and adaptation function of the Framework |
| **When does it start?** | With the first Phase I baseline / governed farm record |
| **When does it end?** | It does not have a universal end while the farm or material claims remain active |
| **Is it a final phase?** | **No.** It runs underneath Phases I, II, and III |
| **Is it the same as MRV?** | **No.** MRV defines how claims become evidence; Phase IV defines the continuous operating use of that evidence |
| **Is it the same as Kokonut Intelligence?** | **No.** Kokonut Intelligence is software/data infrastructure that can implement and automate parts of the workflow |
| **Does every record become public?** | **No.** Publication depends on purpose, privacy, consent, sensitivity, and governance |
| **Does every record need EAS/IPFS?** | **No.** Provenance/storage tools are optional and claim-dependent |
| **Does every farm need an annual impact report?** | **No.** Reporting cadence should match the decision, claim, method, and stakeholder need |
| **Who decides from the evidence?** | The authority appropriate to the decision: local operators, reviewers, funders, Moloch, Guilds, partners, or technical maintainers |
| **What is the core output?** | Better decisions based on increasingly comparable actual records and explicit uncertainty |

## Phase IV connects four different things

Phase IV is strongest when these are kept distinct.

| Layer | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operations** | What happened in the real farm? | Planting, irrigation, harvest, repair, sale, training, incident |
| **Evidence** | What record supports that event or outcome? | Field note, measurement, invoice, soil sample, harvest record |
| **Interpretation** | What does the evidence justify saying? | “Loss rate decreased during this reporting period” |
| **Decision** | What should change next? | Adjust crop density, repair pump, revise forecast, pursue buyer, narrow claim |

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Real farm operation"] --> B["Source record"]
    B --> C["Review / quality control"]
    C --> D["Actual / measurement / derived indicator"]
    D --> E["Interpretation"]
    E --> F["Decision"]
    F --> G["Operational change"]
    G --> A

    C --> H["Publish when appropriate"]
    H --> I["Optional provenance attestation"]
    I --> E
```

> **Phase IV is useful only if evidence can change decisions.**

# The Phase IV operating model

## 1. Capture the right record

Not every farm event deserves the same record.

Useful record families can include:

- farm activity;
- planting / establishment;
- harvest;
- sale / distribution;
- expense / procurement;
- labor / role;
- field note;
- soil sample;
- water measurement;
- vegetation observation;
- biodiversity / survival;
- infrastructure / maintenance;
- incident / risk;
- training / competency;
- milestone / acceptance;
- partner / buyer interaction;
- public-goods delivery;
- certification / compliance status;
- governance / funding decision.

The record should fit the question.

<Info>
  **Collecting more data is not automatically better.** A smaller set of consistent, decision-relevant records can be more useful than a large volume of incomparable observations.
</Info>

---

## 2. Preserve evidence type

Phase IV should keep the Framework's evidence vocabulary visible.

| Evidence type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Documented state** | A recorded current/historical fact | “The farm has 110 hens in the documented implementation” |
| **Observation** | A recorded field condition | “Standing water observed after rainfall” |
| **Measurement** | A quantity produced through a defined method | Soil moisture reading |
| **Derived indicator** | A value computed from source data | NDVI, loss rate, gross margin |
| **Forecast** | A modeled future value | Expected crop revenue |
| **Actual** | An observed operational/financial result | Realized sale price |
| **Attestation** | A signed provenance statement | EAS record pointing to evidence |
| **Interpretation** | A bounded conclusion based on evidence | “Vegetation condition improved in the measured area during the period” |

Do not let a dashboard or report silently convert one type into another.

> **Forecast ≠ actual. Observation ≠ measurement. Measurement ≠ causality. Attestation ≠ certification. Interpretation ≠ raw evidence.**

---

## 3. Use a governed record lifecycle

Kokonut Intelligence currently distinguishes record lifecycle states such as:

> **draft → submitted → verified / rejected → published**

That lifecycle is useful for workflow control.

But the words must be interpreted carefully.

### Verified

Within Kokonut Intelligence, **Verified** means the record passed the applicable internal review workflow.

It does **not** automatically mean:

- independently audited;
- scientifically validated;
- regulator-approved;
- organically certified;
- carbon-verified;
- legally adjudicated.

### Published

**Published** means the record is available to the intended downstream/public surface.

It does not mean the record is:

- complete forever;
- still current;
- free of uncertainty;
- appropriate for every later claim.

### Rejected

A rejected record can be valuable evidence about:

- missing provenance;
- inconsistent units;
- unsupported claims;
- duplicate data;
- invalid method;
- insufficient review.

Phase IV should preserve rejection/quality history rather than hiding it.

---

## 4. Keep freshness visible

A high-quality record can become stale.

Every material current-state claim should make it possible to answer:

- when was the source event?
- when was the record created?
- when was it reviewed?
- when was it published?
- when was it last updated?
- is a newer record expected?
- does the claim still describe current farm state?

### Example

> “The farm has seven jobs.”

That can be a valid documented reference.

For a current employment claim, Phase IV should still ask:

- seven as of when?
- full-time, part-time, seasonal, or role count?
- are the roles still active?
- what changed after the source period?

> **Publication status and freshness are different properties.**

---

## 5. Compare like with like

Longitudinal analysis requires compatibility.

Before comparing records across time, check:

- same farm / plot / asset boundary;
- same unit;
- same method;
- same instrument or documented instrument change;
- same or comparable timing/season;
- same denominator;
- same definition;
- same lifecycle semantics;
- known missing-data periods;
- known intervention changes.

### Method changes should be explicit

If a method changes:

1. record the old method;
2. record the new method;
3. state the change date;
4. explain whether values remain comparable;
5. avoid creating a fake continuous trend when comparability was broken.

---

## 6. Replace forecasts with actuals — without deleting the forecast

Phase IV should preserve the original assumption and the later result.

That enables learning.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Original forecast"] --> B["Operating actual"]
    B --> C["Variance"]
    C --> D["Reason / context"]
    D --> E["Revised forecast"]
    E --> F["Next actual"]
```

Examples include:

- planting quantity;
- survival;
- harvest timing;
- yield;
- loss;
- saleable quantity;
- realized price;
- buyer/channel;
- labor;
- input use;
- cost;
- maintenance;
- public-goods transfer/delivery;
- project milestone.

> **A revised forecast should not erase the evidence that the earlier forecast was wrong.**

Forecast error is part of the farm's learning history.

---

## 7. Maintain the farm — not only the dataset

Continuous operations include physical maintenance.

Phase IV should keep current records for assets such as:

- irrigation;
- pumps;
- tanks / reservoirs;
- nursery;
- biofactory;
- poultry infrastructure;
- tools;
- fencing;
- buildings;
- energy systems;
- monitoring devices;
- storage;
- access roads.

Useful maintenance evidence can include:

- condition;
- service date;
- downtime;
- repair;
- replacement;
- spare parts;
- vendor;
- cost;
- safety issue;
- unresolved defect.

<Warning>
  A farm can have excellent data about infrastructure that is no longer functioning. **Evidence quality is not a substitute for maintenance quality.**
</Warning>

---

## 8. Update the risk register from real events

Phase I starts with risks.

Phase II reveals which ones materialize.

Phase III builds stronger controls.

Phase IV keeps the risk model current.

Examples:

- drought;
- storm/flood;
- disease/pest;
- crop mortality;
- water shortage;
- pump failure;
- buyer loss;
- price decline;
- labor shortage;
- key-person dependency;
- safety incident;
- certification correction;
- legal/land change;
- data gap;
- sensor failure;
- privacy issue;
- methodology weakness;
- funding delay.

For each material risk, update:

- evidence;
- likelihood / uncertainty;
- consequence;
- owner;
- mitigation;
- trigger;
- residual risk;
- status;
- review date.

A risk register that never changes after real incidents is not functioning as a learning tool.

# Evidence quality matters more than elapsed time

The old Phase IV page used a time ladder:

> one month = observations → one year = baseline → three years = patterns → five years = compounding credibility

That is too deterministic.

A baseline can exist on day one if it was collected properly.

Five years of inconsistent or stale records can still be weak evidence.

### Stronger longitudinal evidence depends on

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Comparability** | Are periods measuring the same thing in a compatible way? |
| **Completeness** | Are material gaps visible? |
| **Freshness** | Is the current-state claim recent enough? |
| **Method stability** | Were method changes documented? |
| **Provenance** | Can the record be traced to its source? |
| **Review quality** | Was the appropriate reviewer involved? |
| **Boundary** | Is the farm/plot/population/system clear? |
| **Uncertainty** | Are limitations and missing explanations visible? |
| **Actual-vs-forecast status** | Are models separated from observed results? |
| **Decision relevance** | Did the evidence answer a real operational/reporting question? |

<Info>
  **More time can create more opportunities for comparison. It does not guarantee better evidence.**
</Info>

## Cadence should follow the record and claim

There is no universal Phase IV calendar.

| Record / question | Example cadence |
| --- | --- |
| Field activity | Per event |
| Harvest / sale / loss | Per event |
| Expense / payment | Per transaction / accounting cycle |
| Poultry / recurring output | Daily/weekly aggregation where useful |
| Maintenance | Scheduled + incident-based |
| Soil / water | Method-dependent |
| Remote sensing | Comparable periodic scenes |
| Biodiversity / survival | Seasonal / methodology-dependent |
| Training capability | Program / role / assessment cycle |
| Certification | External-standard timeline |
| Risk | Incident / trigger / reporting period |
| Partner / funder report | Agreement-specific |
| Full farm synthesis | Periodic when enough comparable reviewed evidence exists |

> **Use annual reporting when annual reporting serves the claim or stakeholder—not because Phase IV requires one report every year.**

# Publication, privacy, and provenance

## Not every record should be public

Phase IV should distinguish:

- public evidence;
- partner/funder evidence;
- internal operational evidence;
- restricted personal/commercial/legal evidence.

Publication decisions should consider:

- personal information;
- worker privacy;
- exact locations;
- security;
- buyer/vendor confidentiality;
- legal agreements;
- financial sensitivity;
- conservation sensitivity;
- consent;
- public-interest value.

### Public evidence should be deliberately selected

A public record should answer:

- why is this useful publicly?
- what claim does it support?
- is sensitive information removed?
- is the source/method clear?
- is the reporting period clear?
- is the record current enough?
- is the interpretation bounded?

---

## Kokonut Hub

The **Kokonut Hub** is the public surface for selected published project/farm information.

Use it for:

- current published farm records;
- changeable project state;
- public evidence pointers;
- published operational/impact context.

Do not assume:

> Hub = complete farm database

Some legitimate evidence can remain private, restricted, or unpublished.

---

## EAS on Celo

Kokonut Intelligence can anchor selected evidence/claims through **EAS on Celo**.

An attestation can help preserve:

- signer;
- timestamp;
- schema;
- evidence reference;
- provenance context.

It cannot make weak evidence strong.

It cannot independently prove:

- ecological causality;
- carbon sequestration;
- biodiversity restoration;
- certification;
- profitability;
- legal rights;
- work quality.

> **Attest selectively when durable public provenance adds value.**

---

## IPFS / Filecoin or other durable storage

Content-addressed / durable storage can be useful for:

- reports;
- evidence artifacts;
- datasets;
- media;
- snapshots.

It is not a universal Phase IV requirement.

Integrity and persistence are different from scientific validity.

# Phase IV, MRV, Kokonut Intelligence, and the Hub

These layers should not be collapsed.

| Layer | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Phase IV** | Continuous operational use of evidence, review, learning, maintenance, and adaptation |
| **MRV** | Evidence methodology: how observations/measurements become reviewed claims |
| **Kokonut Intelligence** | Canonical records, lifecycle states, events, analytics, reports, forecasts, scoring, agent/data workflows, attestations |
| **Kokonut Hub** | Public surface for selected published farm/project records |
| **EAS** | Optional public provenance / attestation infrastructure |
| **Framework** | Shared methodology and semantics across farms |

This distinction matters because:

- a software workflow is not the methodology;
- a public page is not the entire evidence base;
- an attestation is not a scientific conclusion;
- an internally verified record is not external certification.

# Phase IV feedback should go to the right authority

Phase IV evidence can inform many decisions.

It should not route every decision to the DAO.

| Evidence / decision | Typical user / authority |
| --- | --- |
| Irrigation adjustment | Local farm operator |
| Crop replacement | Local operator + agronomy expertise |
| Harvest timing | Local operator / quality/market context |
| Maintenance priority | Operator / asset owner / budget authority |
| Buyer/channel change | Applicable farm/operator/legal entity |
| Evidence quality issue | Domain / evidence reviewer |
| Framework method problem | Framework + affected reviewers |
| Guild contribution work | Relevant Guild / contribution reviewers |
| Shared DAO treasury request | Moloch |
| Payment execution | Authorized treasury/SAFE process |
| Technical integration | Authorized technical maintainer |
| Legal/certification issue | Applicable legal/certification process |

<Warning>
  **Evidence can inform authority without transferring authority.** A dashboard, reviewer, token holder, agent, or contributor does not become the farm operator merely because they can see the records.
</Warning>

# Phase IV and AI / agent workflows

Agents can make continuous evidence more usable.

They can help:

- detect missing records;
- flag stale data;
- compare forecast vs. actual;
- identify anomalies;
- draft reports;
- assemble evidence packets;
- summarize risk changes;
- calculate defined indicators;
- check schema consistency;
- propose follow-up questions;
- prepare transaction or attestation drafts within authorized workflows.

They should not autonomously:

- certify ecological outcomes;
- approve farm funding;
- decide land rights;
- change farm operations without operator authority;
- invent missing data;
- convert an estimate into an actual;
- treat internal `Verified` as scientific truth;
- publish sensitive evidence without applicable permission;
- make unsupported causal claims.

> **Agent confidence is not evidence quality.**

# Phase IV across the development phases

| Development function | Phase IV contribution |
| --- | --- |
| **Phase I — Planning & Preparation** | Baseline, provenance, evidence plan, assumptions, risks, initial records |
| **Phase II — Production & Regeneration** | Operating actuals, forecast variance, practice records, outcomes, incidents, adaptation |
| **Phase III — Consolidation & Expansion** | Longitudinal quality, maintenance, traceability, capability, market/certification/expansion evidence |
| **New site / major scope** | Evidence from the existing farm informs a new Phase I without pretending conditions will transfer unchanged |

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    P1["Phase I<br/>baseline + assumptions"] --> P2["Phase II<br/>actual operations"]
    P2 --> P3["Phase III<br/>consolidation / bounded expansion"]
    P3 --> P2
    P3 --> N["New site / material new scope"]
    N --> P1

    P4["Phase IV<br/>continuous evidence + learning"]
    P4 --- P1
    P4 --- P2
    P4 --- P3
```

Phase IV is therefore not a destination.

It is the **memory and feedback function** running across the development lifecycle.

# Adelphi — current Phase IV reference

Adelphi is currently the strongest live reference for Phase IV because it already combines:

- a documented Phase I history;
- active Phase II operations;
- ongoing MRV;
- public Kokonut Hub records;
- selected Celo EAS provenance capability through Kokonut Intelligence;
- remote-sensing / field-record capability;
- forecast models that need actual reconciliation;
- soil / vegetation / biodiversity evidence needs;
- infrastructure that requires maintenance;
- market and certification pathways that can change over time.

### The most important Phase IV task at Adelphi

It is not simply:

> collect more data

It is:

> **keep replacing static assumptions with current, comparable, reviewed records while making uncertainty and stale information visible.**

Examples:

| Static/reference statement | Phase IV question |
| --- | --- |
| Seven jobs documented | What is the current role/employment state? |
| 110 hens documented | What is the current flock/output/mortality state? |
| ~100 eggs/day planning estimate | What do recurring actual production records show? |
| 5 lettuce harvests/year model | What do actual crop cycles show? |
| 15% loss assumption | What are actual loss/rejection rates by crop/cycle? |
| $149,110.87 modeled annual gross crop revenue | What revenue has actually been realized by period? |
| 10% public-goods model assumption | What amount was actually allocated/transferred/used under the applicable arrangement? |
| Organic certification in progress | What is the current certification lifecycle status? |
| Native/at-risk species documented | What do current survival/condition/status records show? |

# Replication learning

A farm's long history can help the next farm.

It still does not prove transferability.

Useful replication evidence includes:

- which fields remained useful;
- which definitions needed adaptation;
- which crop assumptions transferred;
- which failed;
- which practices were locally appropriate;
- what operator capacity was required;
- what infrastructure was reusable;
- what market assumptions changed;
- what data collection was too expensive or unnecessary;
- what governance boundaries held;
- what technical integrations remained compatible.

### Terra Viva

Terra Viva remains a **Phase I planning-stage replication test**.

Phase IV evidence from Adelphi can inform Terra Viva's planning.

It should not be copied as if:

- crops;
- yields;
- prices;
- costs;
- soil response;
- biodiversity response;
- labor;
- buyers;
- public-goods economics;

will be identical.

> **Replication learning transfers questions, structures, and lessons more safely than it transfers conclusions.**

# External pathways: evidence enables diligence, not entitlement

Longitudinal evidence can make a farm easier for an external party to assess.

That does not create an automatic capital pathway.

| Possible external pathway | Relevant evidence | Additional requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organic/certified market | Certification status, traceability, quality, buyer history | Applicable certifier/buyer acceptance |
| Carbon pathway | Standard-specific measurements and methodology | External standard, validation/verification, legal/commercial fit |
| Biodiversity / nature claims | Method-specific ecological evidence | Applicable external methodology/assurance if monetized/certified |
| Grant / impact funding | Delivery, financial, outcome, governance evidence | Funder criteria and diligence |
| Commercial finance | Revenue, cost, cash flow, contracts, assets/liabilities | Lender underwriting/legal structure |
| Insurance | Risk, asset, production, incident history | Insurer underwriting |
| RWA / tokenization exploration | Accurate asset/legal/evidence records | Legal rights, jurisdiction, structure, partner diligence |
| Institutional partnership | Multi-period operations, evidence, governance, risk | Partner-specific diligence |

> **Phase IV can improve diligence readiness. It does not create a right to financing, certification, a credit, a premium, or tokenization.**

## Reporting should be decision-specific

A useful Phase IV report should identify:

- decision / audience;
- farm / boundary;
- reporting period;
- sources;
- actuals vs. forecasts;
- methods;
- material changes;
- missing data;
- stale records;
- incidents;
- risks;
- outcomes;
- uncertainty;
- relevant evidence pointers;
- optional attestations;
- next actions.

### Examples

| Report | Useful when |
| --- | --- |
| Crop-cycle review | Operators need to adjust planting/yield/loss assumptions |
| Financial variance report | Budget/revenue/cash assumptions need revision |
| Maintenance review | Asset reliability affects production |
| Buyer/channel review | Market strategy needs evidence |
| Certification packet | External certification process requires records |
| SDG/EBF synthesis | Reviewed evidence supports development/ecological interpretation |
| Risk review | Material risk changed |
| Grant/partner report | Agreement requires a defined reporting period |
| DAO milestone report | Moloch-authorized funding requires evidence/closeout |
| Replication brief | A new farm needs lessons from existing operations |

Not every farm needs all of these.

## Common failure modes

| Failure mode | Better practice |
| --- | --- |
| “Five years of data proves impact.” | Check method, comparability, gaps, boundary, uncertainty, and actual result |
| “One year is the baseline.” | Establish baseline before/at intervention according to the claim |
| “Published means current.” | Track freshness separately |
| “Verified means scientifically validated.” | Treat `Verified` as the applicable internal workflow state |
| “EAS verifies the ecological claim.” | EAS supports provenance |
| “The Hub contains all evidence.” | Treat Hub as selected public published records |
| “Annual reporting is mandatory.” | Match cadence to claim, agreement, method, or decision |
| “More data is better evidence.” | Prioritize consistency, relevance, method, provenance, and quality |
| “Satellite indices prove regeneration.” | Treat them as derived vegetation indicators |
| “AI synthesis proves the conclusion.” | Review source evidence and method |
| “DAO members should review every farm decision.” | Route decisions by authority |
| “Long history means financing-ready.” | External parties still apply their own legal, financial, technical, and risk diligence |
| “Adelphi evidence transfers to Terra Viva.” | Transfer methodology/questions, then collect Terra Viva's own baseline/actuals |

## Phase IV reviewer checklist

Before using longitudinal farm evidence for a decision or public claim, ask:

- [ ] What decision or claim is this evidence supporting?
- [ ] Is the farm/plot/population/system boundary clear?
- [ ] Is the record an observation, measurement, derived indicator, forecast, actual, attestation, or interpretation?
- [ ] Are forecasts separated from actuals?
- [ ] Is the source/provenance inspectable?
- [ ] Is the record current enough for the claim?
- [ ] Are lifecycle states understood correctly?
- [ ] Is internal `Verified` being mistaken for external certification?
- [ ] Are methods/units comparable across periods?
- [ ] Are method changes documented?
- [ ] Are missing-data periods visible?
- [ ] Are privacy/sensitivity constraints respected?
- [ ] Is publication necessary and authorized?
- [ ] Is EAS/IPFS being used only where useful?
- [ ] Are ecological claims separated from implemented practices?
- [ ] Are causal claims supported by the methodology?
- [ ] Are maintenance and incidents included where they affect the conclusion?
- [ ] Are risk updates current?
- [ ] Is the relevant domain reviewer involved?
- [ ] Is the decision routed to the correct authority?
- [ ] Are agent-generated outputs traceable to source records and reviewed?
- [ ] Is replication being inferred beyond the available multi-farm evidence?
- [ ] Is the next evidence/action explicit?
