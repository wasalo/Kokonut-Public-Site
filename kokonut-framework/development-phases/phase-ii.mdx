---
title: "PHASE II: Production and Regeneration"
icon: "recycle"
---

Phase II is where the farm becomes a farm. After the planning, proposal, infrastructure, training, and soil preparation of [Phase I](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-i), Phase II is when seeds go into the ground, crops begin growing across all three cycle lengths, and the Kokonut Framework's circular farming system starts producing both ecological improvement and community income simultaneously.

**When Phase II begins:** Immediately after Phase I completion criteria are met and the first DAO milestone disbursement is released — triggering infrastructure build-out and the first planting sequence.

**How long Phase II runs:** Phase II is the longest phase in the Framework lifecycle. It runs from first planting until the farm meets Phase III consolidation criteria — typically 2–4 years, covering the establishment period for medium-cycle crops and the beginning of long-cycle crop production. At [Adelphi](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/summary), Phase II is currently active.

**What Phase II produces:**

- First harvests across short and medium cycle crops — cash flow that begins within 30–75 days for fast-growing vegetables
- Continuous MRV data stream — satellite vegetation indices, soil probe readings, and harvest records accumulating the longitudinal dataset that compounds in value over time
- On-chain EAS attestations for every verified milestone — creating the verifiable track record that reduces Project Developer Risk in the annual [CRISP](/kokonut-framework/framework-add-ons/ecological-impact-frameworks) assessment
- Progressive soil health improvement — documented via ground probe readings showing increasing EC and improving VWC between each crop cycle **DAO milestone disbursements:** Phase II is the primary period for KDP milestone disbursements. Treasury funds are released in tranches tied to on-chain verified milestones — not on a calendar schedule. No milestone disbursement is processed without a corresponding EAS-attested MRV event confirming the milestone is complete.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Implementation of Agro-ecological Practices" icon="hand-holding-seedling" iconType="regular">
    Activating the full set of regenerative farming practices that were designed during Phase I and for which the team was trained — applying them consistently across all production zones from the first planting cycle forward. **Crop rotation:** Planned sequences that prevent pest and disease cycle accumulation in any single bed, replenish different soil nutrient profiles across consecutive plantings, and maintain the successional strata structure that makes the farm self-regulating over time.

    **Beneficial plant associations:** Strategic companion planting based on the syntropic strata design — legumes fixing nitrogen alongside nitrogen-demanding crops, aromatic species deterring specific pest species, and ground cover species maintaining living root systems between harvests.

    **Integrated pest management — the syntropic approach:** In the Kokonut Framework, integrated pest management does not mean controlled synthetic pesticide application. It means designing the farm so that pest pressure is managed biologically:

    - **Biodiversity** creates habitat for natural pest predators — birds, beneficial insects, soil organisms — that keep pest populations below economically damaging thresholds
    - **Free-range poultry integration** provides direct pest management through selective grazing — at Adelphi, 110 free-range hens move through the farm managing ground-level pest pressure while depositing fertility
    - **Crop rotation** disrupts pest lifecycle patterns that monoculture would allow to complete and amplify
    - **No synthetic pesticides** are used at any stage — consistent with the first of the [5 Principles of Regeneration](/kokonut-framework/framework-components/framework-features/5-principles-of-regeneration)

    **Organic fertilization:** All fertility inputs are produced on-site from Phase I infrastructure — humic acids and organic urea from poultry manure processing applied per crop cycle; biochar supplementation to beds showing declining EC; cover crop biomass incorporated between cycles.

    **At Adelphi:** All agro-ecological practices were activated at Phase II commencement — the closed-loop fertility cycle (poultry manure → humic acids → beds → forage → poultry) began running from the first planting; beard grass cover crop system was established on all terraced edges; and the 110-hen flock began its integration role across the production zones.

    **MRV connection:** Community analytics logged per crop cycle — crop cycle stage, plant health observations, pest and disease flags, and water analysis. These form the community sensing tier of the MRV stack and provide ground truth for satellite vegetation index readings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Soil Regeneration Initiatives" icon="farm" iconType="regular">
    Actively building soil health across all production zones through the application of complementary regeneration techniques — each addressing a different dimension of soil biology, chemistry, and structure. **Remineralization:** Application of mineral-rich rock materials to replenish trace elements depleted from agricultural soils over time. Rock dust — basalt, granite, zeolite, or mineral-rich rocks found in the local geology — introduces the full spectrum of trace minerals that conventional fertilizers do not supply. At Adelphi, remineralization is integrated directly into the biochar production process: mineral-rich rocks are incorporated during bamboo pyrolysis, producing a biochar that carries both carbon structure and mineral content simultaneously.

    **Soil microbiology enhancement:** Protecting and building the soil food web — the billions of organisms per teaspoon of healthy soil that convert organic matter into plant-available nutrients, suppress pathogens, and build the soil aggregates that give healthy soil its structure. Methods include reducing soil disturbance between cycles, applying humic acids that feed microbial communities, and maintaining the living cover that keeps soil temperature stable enough for microbial activity during dry periods.

    **Ground cover maintenance:** Beard grass and other living cover species maintained continuously across all zones — preventing bare soil exposure that would heat the surface, disrupt soil biology, and allow erosion during rainfall events. The syntropic strata system itself contributes to ground cover through species interactions across all vertical layers.

    **Organic matter incorporation:** Biomass from pruned successional species, harvested crop residues, and cover crop cycling is incorporated into beds between crop cycles — building the soil organic matter that improves water retention, cation exchange capacity, and biological activity with each passing season.

    **At Adelphi:** Remineralization through biochar-mineral incorporation is applied to all beds at the beginning of each crop cycle. Humic acids from the poultry manure processing station are applied per planting. Ground probe readings (EC and VWC) are taken before and after each application — documenting the incremental soil improvement that accumulates across Phase II.

    **MRV connection:** Soil probe readings (volumetric water content, electrical conductivity, soil temperature) logged continuously — the primary quantitative evidence of Phase II soil regeneration progress. Improving EC over successive cycles is the measurable proof that remineralization and organic matter incorporation are working.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Diverse Crop Planting" icon="seedling" iconType="regular">
    Activating all three crop cycle lengths simultaneously — the defining characteristic of Phase II and the core mechanism by which the syntropic farm generates revenue immediately while building toward long-term ecological and financial stability. The three-cycle structure solves the fundamental agricultural investment problem: long-cycle crops with the highest long-term value have the slowest return, but a farm that plants only long-cycle crops generates no income during their establishment period. By planting all three cycles simultaneously, Phase II ensures that short-cycle revenue bridges the establishment gap for medium and long-cycle crops.

    ---

    **Short cycle — fast-growing vegetables**

    Vegetables and biomass crops that provide immediate yields and establish ground-level coverage across the farm's lower strata. These are the crops that generate cash flow within weeks of planting — critical for demonstrating revenue performance during the early DAO milestone review periods.

    _Framework examples:_ Lettuce, tomatoes, spinach, arugula, broccoli, kale, and other fast-growing leafy vegetables and biomass species.

    _At Adelphi:_

    - **Crop:** Lettuce (primary) \+ broccoli, spinach, tomatoes, arugula
    - **Planting density:** 12 plants/m² across 25 m² beds × 19 beds × 10 plots
    - **First harvest:** 30–75 days after planting (loose-leaf varieties faster, head varieties longer)
    - **Harvest cadence:** 5 cycles per year (conservative planning basis)
    - **Yield per harvest:** 48,450 units after 15% loss adjustment
    - **Revenue per harvest:** $26,647–$30,523 at $0.55–$0.63/unit
    - **Annual revenue:** ~\$133,237

    ---

    **Medium cycle — production and regeneration balance**

    Crops that take 6–12 months to first harvest but establish strong root systems, provide mid-canopy structure, and produce for multiple successive seasons without replanting. Medium-cycle crops are the bridge between the immediate cash flow of short-cycle vegetables and the permanent anchor income of long-cycle trees.

    _Framework examples:_ Passion fruit, Indian yam, plantain, banana, and other medium-duration species suited to the farm's specific climate.

    _At Adelphi:_

    - **Crops:** Passion fruit (primary) \+ Indian yam
    - **Passion fruit planting:** 70 plants/plot × 8 plots = 560 plants at 3×3m spacing
    - **First harvest:** 6–12 months after planting
    - **Annual production per plant:** 85 fruits (after 15% loss adjustment)
    - **Total annual production:** 47,600 fruits = 3,661 nets
    - **Annual revenue:** ~$11,019 at $3.01/net

    ---

    **Long cycle — permanent agroforestry structure**

    Perennial trees and long-duration crops that take 3–5 years to first harvest but produce for decades — building the permanent agroforestry canopy that gives the farm its biodiversity structure, carbon sequestration capacity, and eventual anchor income. Long-cycle crops are the foundation of the Kokonut Network's cooperative model: each \$vKKN token is backed 1:1 by a real coconut tree.

    _Framework examples:_ Coconut, cacao, timber species, hardwood agroforestry species, and other perennial trees suited to the farm's climate and soil profile.

    _At Adelphi:_

    - **Primary crop:** Coconut (Cocos nucifera)
    - **Planting:** 12 trees/plot × 8 plots = 96 trees at 7×7m spacing
    - **First harvest:** 3–5 years after planting
    - **Annual production at maturity:** 64 coconuts/tree × 96 trees = 6,144 coconuts
    - **Annual revenue at maturity:** ~$4,854 at $0.79/coconut
    - **Production life:** Up to 20 years — replanted at end of productive life
    - **Additional value:** Coconut husks → on-site fertilizer; wood → furniture and materials

    ---

    **Combined Phase II revenue trajectory at Adelphi:**

    | **Month** | **Revenue source** | **Cumulative annual run rate** |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Month 1–3 | Phase I infrastructure build | \$0 |
    | Month 3–4 | First lettuce harvest (30–75 days) | ~\$133,237/yr begins |
    | Month 6–12 | First passion fruit harvest | \+\$11,019/yr |
    | Year 1\+ | Poultry eggs continuous | Additional stream |
    | Year 3–5 | First coconut harvest | \+\$4,854/yr at maturity |
    | **Year 5\+** | **All cycles at full production** | **~\$149,110/yr** |

    **MRV connection:** Every harvest cycle is recorded via `POST /farms/{farm_id}/harvests` in the Farm Registry API — logging crop type, plot, yield, loss rate, and sale price. These records are the economic evidence layer of the MRV pipeline and feed directly into the annual EBF Economic Impact dimension.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Phase II MRV milestones

Phase II generates a continuous stream of on-chain EAS attestations — each triggered by a verified farm event. These attestations are what transform farm activity from self-reported progress into cryptographically verifiable milestones.

| **Milestone** | **Trigger** | **EAS attestation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First planting complete | All crop beds planted across three cycle lengths | `farm_event` attestation: `event_type: first_planting` |
| First MRV data submitted | First satellite MRV payload accepted by Farm Registry API | `mrv_event` attestation: `is_attested: true` |
| First harvest recorded | First harvest record submitted and confirmed | `farm_event` attestation: `event_type: first_harvest` |
| Soil improvement documented | EC readings showing measurable improvement vs Phase I baseline | `mrv_event` attestation: ground probe data anchored |
| 6-month production review | EBF interim report submitted to Data Hub | `impact_event` attestation |
| Annual EBF report | Full Environmental, Economic, Social, Sustainability report | `impact_event` attestation: annual |

All Phase II attestations are anchored on Gnosis Chain and published to [hub.kokonut.network/projects/41](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41).

## Phase II completion criteria

Phase II is complete when the farm has demonstrated sustained multi-cycle production with verified MRV data — not simply when a time period has elapsed.

| **Criterion** | **How it's verified** |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum 3 full short-cycle harvest records | Farm Registry API `/harvests` endpoint — at least 3 complete lettuce (or equivalent) cycles logged and attested |
| Medium-cycle crops established and productive | First passion fruit or Indian yam harvest recorded and attested |
| Soil health improvement documented | Phase II EC and VWC readings show measurable improvement vs Phase I baseline across at least 2 consecutive cycles |
| First annual EBF report published | Environmental, Economic, Social, Sustainability dimensions all documented and anchored as EAS attestation |
| CRISP annual assessment complete | All 5 risk factors scored and included in EBF report |
| Long-cycle crops established | Coconut (or equivalent) trees confirmed healthy and growing via Silvi GPS health logs and NDRE satellite monitoring |

## Advancing to Phase III

Phase II completion triggers the advancement to [Phase III — Consolidation and Expansion](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-iii). The transition is recorded as an EAS attestation on-chain and reflected in the Farm Registry API `status` field — making the phase transition verifiable and timestamped.

Phase III begins when the farm is producing reliably across all three cycle lengths, has a verified MRV track record, and is ready to pursue organic certification, expand production capacity, and develop the long-term market relationships that will sustain the operation beyond the DAO's initial funding horizon.

> Phase IV MRV continues running throughout Phase II without interruption. Satellite monitoring, soil probe readings, and community analytics accumulate continuously — the data record that makes Phase III's consolidation case to institutional partners is built during Phase II's production period.
