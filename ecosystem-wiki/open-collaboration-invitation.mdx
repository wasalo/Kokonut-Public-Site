# Contribute useful work before you contribute capital.

Kokonut Network is an open collaboration ecosystem around regenerative agriculture.

You do **not** need to become a DAO member, buy a token, or contribute capital to begin.

You can start by improving documentation, reviewing a farm record, researching a method, writing code, helping with MRV, strengthening a proposal, contributing agronomic knowledge, supporting a partnership, or solving another clearly defined problem.

> **The best first contribution is small enough to review, useful enough to matter, and documented well enough that someone else can learn from it.**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Find a place to contribute" icon="code-branch" href="https://github.com/wasalo/Kokonut-Public-Site/issues">
    Start with an issue, a documentation gap, a bounded research question, a Guild task, or another contribution that can be reviewed.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Understand the live context first" icon="seedling" href="/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/summary">

  </Card>

<Info>
  **Contribution and capital are separate paths.** The Moloch DAO coordinates capital and treasury decisions. Guilds and project workflows coordinate useful work. A contributor can begin without capital.
</Info>

## How contribution works

Kokonut's contribution model is designed around **useful work + evidence + review**, not visibility or proximity to the core team.

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Understand the context" icon="book-open-reader" iconType="regular">
    Read the relevant documentation, inspect live records when available, and understand whether the system you want to change is live, evolving, developing, or proposed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="2. Choose a bounded contribution" icon="bullseye" iconType="regular">
    Define one useful outcome: fix a page, review a forecast, validate a record, write a research note, improve a method, build a small tool, or prepare a proposal draft.
  </Step>

  <Step title="3. Open an issue when the change is material" icon="message-question" iconType="regular">
    Architecture, methodology, governance, MRV, schemas, APIs, navigation, contract behavior, and other shared primitives should be discussed before implementation.
  </Step>

  <Step title="4. Do the work in a reviewable form" icon="hammer" iconType="regular">
    Produce something another person can inspect: a pull request, report, dataset, model, field record, design, analysis, proposal, or documented coordination outcome.
  </Step>

  <Step title="5. Submit evidence and assumptions" icon="clipboard-check" iconType="regular">
    Explain what changed, why it matters, what sources support it, what assumptions remain, and what could be affected.
  </Step>

  <Step title="6. Review before recognition" icon="user-check" iconType="regular">
    Relevant contributors, Guilds, maintainers, farm operators, or governance participants review the work according to its domain and impact.
  </Step>

  <Step title="7. Build a contribution record" icon="clipboard-check" iconType="regular">
    Reviewed work can become part of a contributor's history: what was done, who did it, what evidence supports it, and which domain it belongs to.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Recognition is not automatic.** Completed and reviewed contributions can qualify for Guild Points under the relevant Guild's rules. Significant network-level recognition can require a Moloch DAO proposal. Guild Points themselves are still a developing coordination system.
</Note>

## Good first contributions

A good first contribution usually does not require governance authority or a large budget.

| Area | Good first contribution | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Documentation** | Fix an outdated claim, broken link, confusing section, diagram, or onboarding flow | Pull request, before/after explanation, source links |
| **Agronomy** | Review a crop assumption, planting practice, soil method, or farm-operation document | Technical note, farm context, references, operator feedback |
| **MRV / Impact** | Validate an indicator, improve evidence requirements, review an SDG mapping, or identify missing data | Method note, dataset, calculation, provenance |
| **Software** | Fix a small issue, improve a validator, add a test, document an API, or prototype a useful view | PR, tests, screenshots, example payload |
| **Finance** | Review a forecast assumption, budget line, allocation model, or treasury-reporting format | Spreadsheet/model, assumptions, sensitivity notes |
| **Governance** | Improve a proposal template, review checklist, process explanation, or decision record | Draft, rationale, affected process, reviewer notes |
| **Community** | Improve onboarding, summarize a working session, document a partnership lead, or create an educational resource | Published artifact, notes, links, participant feedback |
| **Research** | Answer one concrete question relevant to a farm, method, regulation, market, or technical design | Sources, methodology, findings, limitations |

> **Small, finished, reviewable work is more useful than a large idea nobody can validate.**

## Capital participation is a different path

Some contributors may also want to participate in Kokonut's capital governance.

That path runs through the [Kokonut Moloch DAO](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/kokonut-moloch-dao), not through ordinary contribution activity.

Capital participation can involve:

- stablecoin tribute;
- membership proposals;
- \$vKKN governance rights;
- treasury voting;
- rage-quit protections;
- proposal sponsorship and review.

Contribution participation can involve:

- useful work;
- contribution records;
- Guild review;
- Guild Points when awarded under the relevant rules;
- potential DAO-level recognition for significant work.

These paths interact, but they should not be confused.

<Info>
  You can contribute to Kokonut without joining the Moloch DAO. You can also become a DAO member without being qualified to make farm, technical, scientific, or domain-specific decisions outside the scope of capital governance.
</Info>

## Contribution principles

The collaboration model follows the same standards used across the Knowledge Base.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Evidence over claims" icon="magnifying-glass-chart">
    Material claims should link to records, methods, sources, or clearly stated assumptions.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Actuals are not forecasts" icon="calculator">
    Estimates, scenarios, targets, forecasts, and modeled outcomes must remain distinguishable from recorded results.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Local context matters" icon="map">
    A method that works at Adelphi should not automatically be imposed on another farm without understanding its ecology, people, markets, and regulation.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Compatibility matters" icon="database">
    Schema, API, MRV, attestation, and shared-method changes should explain migration and backward-compatibility implications.
  </Card>

  <Card title="AI does not remove responsibility" icon="robot">
    AI-assisted work is welcome, but contributors remain responsible for factual accuracy, evidence, review, and the consequences of public claims.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Open should create capacity" icon="unlock">
    Prefer documentation, standards, tools, and workflows that make future contributors and communities more capable rather than more dependent.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Work that needs extra review

Some contributions can affect live farms, governance, data compatibility, or public claims.

Open an issue **before implementation** when changing:

- the Common Data Schema;
- MRV payloads or evidence requirements;
- attestation structures;
- governance rules or proposal processes;
- token or contract behavior;
- public farm-data interpretation;
- impact methodology;
- financial or harvest assumptions used across multiple pages or tools;
- navigation or major information architecture;
- API contracts or shared data models.

For those changes, explain:

1. what problem you are solving;
2. who or what is affected;
3. why the current approach is insufficient;
4. whether the change is backward compatible;
5. what evidence supports the proposal;
6. how it should be reviewed or migrated.

This protects live systems from well-intentioned but incompatible changes.

## What a strong contribution looks like

Before submitting work, ask:

- **Is the problem clear?**
- **Is the scope bounded?**
- **Did I use the current repository and live records rather than stale assumptions?**
- **Are facts, estimates, and opinions distinguishable?**
- **Can another person inspect my evidence?**
- **Did I preserve local context?**
- **Did I avoid overstating the maturity of developing infrastructure?**
- **If I changed a shared primitive, did I explain compatibility?**
- **Did I identify who should review the work?**
- **Does the contribution leave the ecosystem easier to understand, operate, verify, or improve?**

If the answer is yes, the contribution is probably ready for review.
