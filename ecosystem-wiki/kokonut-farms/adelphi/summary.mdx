# Adelphi is Kokonut's first live reference farm.

Adelphi is a women-led, locally operated syntropic farm in **Gonzalo, Sabana Grande de Boyá, Monte Plata, Dominican Republic**, founded and operated by sisters **Yanny and Neury Hernández**.

It is where Kokonut's ideas meet real land: public-goods funding, regenerative agriculture, local operations, community infrastructure, farm records, MRV, forecasts, and shared Framework practices.

Adelphi should be read as a **reference implementation**, not as proof that every future farm will reproduce the same economics, ecological outcomes, governance dynamics, or community effects.

## How to read claims about Adelphi

The farm pages use the evidence vocabulary established in the [MRV methodology](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification).

| Claim type | Example at Adelphi | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Documented state** | 15,725 m² mapped area, 110 hens, founders/operators | A recorded fact or current project attribute |
| **Measurement** | Soil value, mapped area, harvest quantity, GPS point | A value captured through a defined observation or measurement process |
| **Derived indicator** | NDVI, NDRE, ReCI, MSAVI | A value calculated from source data and requiring methodological context |
| **Forecast / model** | Projected lettuce yield or annual crop revenue | A planning scenario based on assumptions, not an achieved result |
| **Attestation / reviewed claim** | Published or attested farm record | A claim with review/provenance context; not automatic scientific certification |
| **Interpretation** | SDG mapping, EBF profile, CRISP review | A conclusion produced by applying a framework to underlying evidence |

> **The most important distinction on Adelphi pages is actual vs. forecast.** A planned yield, modeled revenue, or projected impact remains a forecast until supported by recorded outcomes.

## The founders and origin

Yanny and Neury Hernández are sisters, single mothers, landowners, and the founders/operators of Adelphi.

Their family history is rooted in agriculture. As adults, Yanny built a career in the pharmaceutical industry and Neury became a professional manicurist, while both retained a long-term goal of returning to land stewardship.

In **2022**, they purchased land in Gonzalo with the intention of building an agricultural future tied to family legacy and community benefit.

The major barrier documented in their story was not whether the land could produce. It was the capital and infrastructure required to turn ownership and intention into an operating farm.

[Public Nouns Proposal #69](https://publicnouns.wtf/vote/69) helped fund critical infrastructure, allowing Adelphi to develop as both a productive farm and a public-good-aligned learning site.

Adelphi combines production, ecological restoration, on-site inputs, animals, and community infrastructure.


The documented system includes:

- short-cycle vegetables;
- passion fruit and Indian yam;
- coconut trees and native agroforestry species;

- biological input production;
- syntropic plots;
- a multipurpose training and community gazebo;

## Production is organized across multiple time horizons

Adelphi uses a diversified production model rather than depending on one crop cycle.

| Production horizon | Examples | Role in the farm model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short cycle** | Lettuce and companion vegetables | Faster harvest cycles and near-term operating revenue potential |
| **Medium cycle** | Passion fruit, Indian yam | Seasonal diversification |
| **Long cycle** | Coconut and native agroforestry species | Perennial production and long-term ecological structure |
| **Continuous** | Poultry | Eggs, nutrient cycling, manure inputs, and an additional production stream |

This mix is intended to connect near-term production with longer-term perennial systems and ecological regeneration.

Actual harvest performance should be evaluated from farm records rather than inferred from the planting model.

## Forecast snapshot — not actual performance

The detailed [Crops & Harvest Forecast](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/adelphi/crops-and-harvest-forecast) models production and revenue using explicit assumptions.

| Forecast stream | Planning assumption | Forecast annual output / revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lettuce** | 48,450 units per harvest × 5 harvests | 242,250 units / **\$133,237.50** |
| **Passion fruit** | 560 plants, 85 fruits/plant | 47,600 fruits / 3,661 nets / **\$11,019.61** |
| **Coconut** | 96 trees, 64 coconuts/tree at maturity | 6,144 coconuts / **\$4,853.76** |
| **Poultry eggs** | 110 hens, ~100 eggs/day estimate | ~36,500 eggs/year; revenue not included in crop total |
| **Projected gross crop revenue** | Crop model only | **~\$149,110.87/year** |

<Warning>
  These are **forecasts**, not recorded harvest or revenue actuals. Weather, pests, soil conditions, labor, market access, timing, prices, and operational execution can materially change results.
</Warning>

The forecast currently uses a **15% crop-loss assumption** and **5 lettuce harvests per year**.

The planning model also assigns **10% of projected gross crop revenue** to public-goods activities. At modeled performance, that would equal approximately **\$14,911.09/year**.

That value should be described as a **forecasted allocation**, not as money already realized or distributed.

## How evidence moves through Adelphi

The farm uses several evidence sources because different claims require different kinds of support.

| Evidence source | What it can support | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Field observations** | Crop condition, disease flags, operational notes, local context | Human observations should preserve authorship and review context |
| **Ground measurements** | Soil moisture, electrical conductivity, soil temperature | Units, placement, calibration, and reporting period matter |
| **Remote sensing** | Vegetation and land-change indicators such as NDVI, NDRE, ReCI, MSAVI | Derived indices do not directly prove yield, carbon, or biodiversity |
| **Per-plant GPS** | Location, status, phenology, species records | A GPS record proves location/provenance, not ecological impact by itself |
| **Harvest records** | Actual production and forecast reconciliation | Planned production should never be mixed with harvest actuals |
| **Published Hub records** | Public project and farm information | Publication state and timestamp matter |
| **EAS attestations on Celo** | Provenance for selected claims and evidence references | Attestation does not equal external scientific certification |
| **EBF / CRISP / SDG reporting** | Interpretation of reviewed evidence | Interpretation is only as strong as the underlying records and method |

## Framework status

The current public documentation places Adelphi in **Phase II — Production & Regeneration**.

| Framework phase | Documented status | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Phase I — Planning & Preparation](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-i) | **Complete** | Baseline planning, infrastructure, soil preparation, training, and setup |
| [Phase II — Production & Regeneration](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-ii) | **Active** | Production, regenerative practices, crop systems, and ongoing farm operations |
| [Phase III — Consolidation & Expansion](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-iii) | **Upcoming** | Market consolidation, certification, biodiversity expansion, and broader growth |
| [Phase IV — Continuous MRV](/kokonut-framework/development-phases/phase-iv) | **Ongoing across phases** | Records, measurements, evidence review, reporting, and verification workflows |

<Info>
  Phase IV should not be interpreted as a final stage that starts after production. MRV runs across the farm lifecycle and becomes more useful as time-series records accumulate.
</Info>

### What Adelphi does not prove yet

- that every future farm can use the same crop model;
- that forecast revenues will be realized;
- that every reported ecological benefit has been independently certified;
- that on-chain attestations validate scientific conclusions;
- that a second farm will replicate Adelphi without new local adaptation;
- that the current Framework has already solved multi-farm scaling.

> **Adelphi's strongest role is as a live place to test, measure, learn, and improve.**
