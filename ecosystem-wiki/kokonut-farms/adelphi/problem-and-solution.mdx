# Adelphi starts with one documented problem: land and intent were not enough.

Yanny and Neury Hernández purchased land in Gonzalo, Monte Plata in **2022** with the intention of building a productive agricultural future.

The central barrier documented in their story was clear:

> **They had land, commitment, and a farming vision, but lacked the capital and infrastructure required to turn that land into an operating farm.**

[Public Nouns Proposal #69](https://publicnouns.wtf/vote/69) helped finance critical infrastructure and created the conditions for Adelphi to move from intention into implementation.

Around that core problem, the project is also designed to address six additional priorities: **regenerative education, local food production, land regeneration, continuity of farming knowledge, community space, and women-led agricultural leadership.**

## The documented starting point

The strongest problem statement at Adelphi comes directly from the founders' history.

Yanny and Neury:

- grew up with a family connection to farming;
- built careers outside agriculture;
- purchased land in Gonzalo in 2022;
- intended to build a productive farm tied to family legacy and community benefit;
- encountered difficulty financing the infrastructure required to make the land productive.

This creates a narrower and more defensible local problem:

> **How can locally owned land move from undercapitalized potential to productive regenerative infrastructure without removing the founders from the center of the project?**

Adelphi's first answer was infrastructure.

Public Nouns funding supported the transition from owned land toward an operating farm with production systems, poultry, nursery capacity, soil inputs, water and farm infrastructure, community space, and public documentation.

## Seven areas Adelphi is designed to address

The original Adelphi documentation describes seven local challenges.

A more precise framing is to treat them as **one documented constraint plus six intervention priorities**.

| Area | Current evidence status | Adelphi response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Capital and employment** | Capital/infrastructure constraint is directly documented; 7 jobs are documented | Fund physical farm capacity and operate a productive agricultural system |
| **2. Regenerative education** | Education infrastructure is documented; broader learning outcomes require program records | Use the farm and gazebo for practical regenerative-agriculture learning |
| **3. Local food production** | Crops and poultry are documented; community food-access outcomes require distribution data | Produce diversified vegetables, fruits, perennial crops, and eggs |
| **4. Land regeneration and biodiversity** | Nursery, syntropic systems, biochar, species work, and MRV methods are documented | Use regenerative practices while monitoring ecological indicators over time |
| **5. Farming-knowledge continuity** | Founder story and education intent are documented; intergenerational outcomes require participation evidence | Create a place where agricultural knowledge can be practiced and shared |
| **6. Community space** | Multipurpose gazebo is documented; community-capacity outcomes require event and participation records | Provide physical infrastructure for meetings, workshops, meals, and learning |
| **7. Women-led agricultural leadership** | Women-led ownership and operations are documented | Keep Yanny and Neury central to land stewardship, operations, and public leadership |

This framing avoids treating an intended outcome as if it has already been measured.

## 2. Regenerative education → a living learning environment

Adelphi is designed to function as more than a production site.

The documented farm includes a **multipurpose gazebo** used as a meeting, dining, training, and demonstration space.

The broader education model can include topics such as:

- syntropic farming;
- agroforestry;
- native-species propagation;
- biochar;
- soil health;
- poultry integration;
- farm records;
- MRV literacy.

The physical capacity for education is already present.

The educational **outcome** should be measured separately.

Useful evidence can include:

- number of workshops held;
- attendance;
- participant type;
- curriculum or topic;
- repeat participation;
- operator or participant feedback;
- skills applied after training.

> **A training space is documented infrastructure. Community learning is an outcome that should be demonstrated through program records.**

## 3. Local food production → diversified farm output

Adelphi is designed around multiple production horizons.

| Horizon | Examples | Current role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short cycle** | Lettuce and companion vegetables | Faster production cycles |
| **Medium cycle** | Passion fruit, Indian yam | Seasonal diversification |
| **Long cycle** | Coconut and agroforestry species | Perennial production and ecological structure |
| **Continuous** | Laying hens | Eggs, nutrient cycling, and an additional farm output |

This supports a clear claim:

**Adelphi is building diversified food-production capacity.**

It does not yet automatically support the stronger claim that local food insecurity has been reduced.

That would require evidence such as:

- realized harvest quantities;
- where products were sold or distributed;
- community-access records;
- repeat buyers or beneficiaries;
- price and affordability data;
- household or community food-access research.

## 4. Land regeneration → practices plus measured ecological change

Adelphi uses practices designed around regeneration:

- syntropic planting;
- agroforestry;
- organic inputs;
- bamboo-derived biochar;
- ground cover;
- native and at-risk species propagation;
- poultry integration;
- vegetation monitoring.

Those practices are documented.

The ecological outcomes require a stronger evidence standard.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Regenerative practice"] --> B["Farm record"]
    B --> C["Repeated measurement"]
    C --> D["Trend over time"]
    D --> E["Interpretation"]
    E --> F["Supported ecological claim"]
```

For example:

- applying biochar is a **documented practice**;
- a soil measurement is **evidence**;
- a multi-period soil trend is a **derived result**;
- claiming improved soil function is an **interpretation** that depends on method and baseline.

Likewise, planting native species is not the same as demonstrating increased biodiversity across the farm.

[Read the MRV evidence standard →](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification)

<Warning>
  Adelphi should not describe carbon sequestration, biodiversity restoration, soil improvement, or other ecological outcomes as fully delivered solely because regenerative practices are present. Outcomes require baselines, repeated evidence, and appropriate interpretation.
</Warning>

Yanny and Neury's background connects Adelphi to a family history of agriculture.

The farm is also designed to create a setting where other people can encounter and practice land stewardship.

## 6. Community space → physical infrastructure for coordination

The Adelphi gazebo is a concrete piece of social infrastructure.

It can support:

- meetings;
- meals;
- workshops;
- demonstrations;
- children's activities;
- farmer training;
- visitor education;
- community coordination.

That makes **“Adelphi has dedicated community and training space”** a strong documented claim.

Stronger statements—such as improved social cohesion, stronger community capacity, or intergenerational connection—require evidence about how the space is actually used.

Useful records include:

- event count;
- attendance;
- participant groups;
- recurring programs;
- partner organizations;
- participant feedback;
- outcomes from specific training activities.

## 7. Gender barriers → documented women-led ownership and operations

One of Adelphi's clearest social characteristics is not a forecast.

It is the project's current leadership structure.

Adelphi is founded and operated by **Yanny and Neury Hernández**.

The repository documents them as sisters, single mothers, landowners, founders, and operators.

This supports a grounded claim:

> **Adelphi is a women-led agricultural project with women directly connected to land ownership, operations, infrastructure, and public leadership.**

That is different from claiming that Adelphi has already reduced gender inequality across the surrounding community.

A broader gender-impact claim would require evidence such as:

- participation of women in farm employment;
- training participation;
- decision-making roles;
- income or economic-control outcomes;
- access to land, capital, or technical opportunity;
- change over time.

## How the responses connect

Adelphi is designed as an integrated farm system, but integration should not be confused with proven causality.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    PN["Public Nouns #69"] --> INFRA["Physical farm infrastructure"]

    INFRA --> PROD["Diversified production"]
    INFRA --> JOBS["Farm employment"]
    INFRA --> EDU["Training + community space"]
    INFRA --> ECO["Regenerative practices + nursery"]

    PROD --> RECORDS["Farm records"]
    JOBS --> RECORDS
    EDU --> RECORDS
    ECO --> RECORDS

    RECORDS --> MRV["MRV + review"]
    MRV --> LEARN["Evidence + learning"]
    LEARN --> OPS["Operational improvements"]
    LEARN --> FRAMEWORK["Framework improvements"]
```

This is the strongest current version of the Adelphi value loop:

**fund infrastructure → operate the farm → record what happens → review evidence → improve operations and shared methods.**

Claims about household income, food security, biodiversity, education outcomes, or community resilience belong downstream of that loop and require their own evidence.

## Evidence standard for the seven areas

| Area | Strong current claim | Evidence needed for a stronger outcome claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Capital / employment** | Public funding supported infrastructure; 7 jobs are documented | Payroll, job duration, local hiring, household-income change |
| **Education** | Training/community space exists | Event logs, attendance, curriculum, feedback, skills outcomes |
| **Food production** | Diversified crops and poultry are part of the farm system | Harvest actuals, sales/distribution records, food-access data |
| **Land regeneration** | Regenerative practices and biodiversity infrastructure exist | Baselines, repeated soil/vegetation/biodiversity measurements |
| **Knowledge continuity** | Farm methods are practiced and documented | Participation records, repeat training, intergenerational involvement |
| **Community space** | Multipurpose gazebo exists | Usage records, recurring programs, participant evidence |
| **Women-led leadership** | Yanny and Neury own/operate the project | Broader gender outcomes require participation, income, and opportunity data |

<Info>
  This table is intentionally conservative. It gives future farm reporting a clear path from **implemented intervention → recorded activity → measured outcome → supported impact claim**.
</Info>

### What still needs evidence over time

- realized farm income and household-level economic effects;
- local food-access outcomes;
- workshop and training outcomes;
- changes in soil function;
- changes in biodiversity;
- long-term employment quality and durability;
- community-capacity effects;
- broader gender-equity outcomes;
- causal attribution between any one intervention and an observed result.

> **Adelphi is most credible when it shows the intervention clearly, measures the outcome separately, and leaves room for the evidence to challenge the original assumption.**

## Relationship to the Kokonut model

Adelphi's role is not to prove that one farm design should be copied everywhere.

It helps answer a more useful question:

> **Can public-goods capital, local operators, regenerative farm design, structured evidence, and reusable open infrastructure work together around one real farm?**

The current evidence shows that these components are being connected at Adelphi.

Future Kokonut farms still need their own:

- local diagnosis;
- operators;
- baseline;
- crop and infrastructure plan;
- funding structure;
- evidence requirements;
- community priorities;
- market assumptions;
- risk review.

Adelphi contributes a reference point, not a universal prescription.
