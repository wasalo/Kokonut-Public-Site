# Adelphi began with two sisters who wanted to return to the land.

Adelphi is Kokonut's first live reference farm, founded and operated by sisters **Yanny and Neury Hernández** in Gonzalo, Sabana Grande de Boyá, Monte Plata, Dominican Republic.

Before the farm became a Framework implementation, an MRV site, or a public data source, it began as a personal decision:

> **Yanny and Neury wanted to build an agricultural future on land they owned, connected to a family history of farming.**

This page tells that story.

It does not use the founders' story as proof that every intended social, ecological, or economic outcome has already been achieved. Those claims belong in the farm's operating records, MRV, forecasts, and evidence pages.

## A family memory that survived city life

Yanny and Neury's relationship with agriculture did not begin with Kokonut Network.

The repository describes them as growing up in a farming family and spending childhood vacations at their grandparents' countryside home in the Dominican Republic.

That memory remained part of their lives even as their adult careers moved in different directions.

- **Yanny** worked in the pharmaceutical industry.
- **Neury** became a professional manicurist.

Their return to agriculture was therefore not described as an outside organization assigning them a farm.

It was a decision to reconnect with something already present in their family history.

> **The land came before the coordination infrastructure. The founders came before the Framework.**

That ordering matters to the way Kokonut now describes Adelphi: shared infrastructure should support local operators rather than replace them.

---

## 2022: from intention to land ownership

In **2022**, Yanny and Neury purchased land in Gonzalo.

The repository describes their intention as building a future where they could:

- work with the land;
- preserve a family relationship with agriculture;
- build something useful for their household;
- create benefits that could extend into the surrounding community.

Buying the land was a major commitment.

It did not, however, make the farm operational.

The next challenge was financing.

---

## The gap between owning land and operating a farm

The strongest problem documented in Adelphi's origin story is not a generalized claim about all rural communities.

It is specific:

> **Yanny and Neury owned the land but lacked the capital and infrastructure required to turn it into a working farm.**

Moving from land ownership to production required resources for areas such as:

- land preparation;
- water and irrigation systems;
- tools;
- planting;
- physical infrastructure;
- early operating support.

This is the human version of the coordination problem described elsewhere in Kokonut.

Capital alone does not create a durable farm, but without capital, many of the physical conditions for production cannot begin.

## Public Nouns #69: the turning point

[Public Nouns Proposal #69](https://publicnouns.wtf/vote/69) helped fund critical Adelphi infrastructure.

The significance of that event is practical.

Before funding:

**land + intent + local operators**

After funding, the project could begin building:

**land + operators + infrastructure + production systems + public documentation**

The proposal did not automatically prove that the farm would be profitable, regenerative, educational, or replicable.

It created the conditions for those questions to be tested in the real world.

## Why the name Adelphi matters

The existing project narrative explains **Adelphi** as a reference to the relationship between the two sisters.

That meaning fits the way the farm is presented throughout the repository:

- family before infrastructure;
- local operators before network abstractions;
- cooperation rather than replacement;
- shared work rooted in a specific relationship to land.

Because the current repository is the source for this naming explanation, this page does not attempt to turn that project meaning into a linguistic claim beyond what the documentation supports.

## From a personal project to a public-good-aligned farm

The founders' original motivation was personal and practical.

As the farm developed, Kokonut documentation expanded the project around additional goals:

- diversified food production;
- regenerative farming practices;
- biodiversity and nursery work;
- poultry;
- community and training infrastructure;
- public farm records;
- MRV;
- reusable learning for future farms.

Those later layers matter, but they should not erase the origin.

Adelphi is not primarily a story about technology.

It is a story about locally owned land that gained access to enough coordination infrastructure to begin operating.

## Community purpose: intent first, outcomes second

The repository describes Adelphi as a **community-first** project.

It also identifies nearby communities, including the batey and Haty community, within the farm's intended local context.

The farm's community-oriented design includes:

- a multipurpose gazebo;
- workshops and demonstrations;
- agricultural education;
- nursery propagation;
- seedling distribution;
- a space for meetings and gathering.

Those intentions are documented.

Broader community outcomes still need records.

| Project intent | Evidence needed to demonstrate the outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Teach regenerative agriculture | Workshop records, attendance, curriculum, feedback, follow-up |
| Create a place for community gathering | Event logs, usage records, recurring programs |
| Share native or at-risk plants | Nursery inventory, distribution logs, recipient/destination records |
| Support local food access | Harvest, sales/distribution, buyer and community-access records |
| Preserve agricultural knowledge | Participation evidence, repeat learning, intergenerational involvement |

<Info>
  **The story can explain why these goals exist. It cannot prove they were achieved.** That distinction is now consistent across the Adelphi farm pages.
</Info>

## Women-led ownership is a documented fact

Yanny and Neury are not symbolic beneficiaries of the project.

The repository documents them as:

- sisters;
- single mothers;
- landowners;
- founders;
- farm operators.

This makes women-led ownership and operations one of the clearest social characteristics of Adelphi.

It supports a grounded statement:

> **Women are directly connected to the land, project leadership, and farm operations at Adelphi.**

It does not by itself prove a broader reduction in gender inequality across the surrounding community.

That broader outcome would require additional evidence on employment, participation, income, access to opportunity, and decision-making over time.

## Ecology is part of the stewardship story — but outcomes need measurement

The repository connects Adelphi's human story to:

- syntropic farming;
- native and at-risk species propagation;
- biochar;
- agroforestry;
- ground cover;
- poultry integration;
- nursery work.

These practices reflect a stewardship intention: use the farm productively while improving the conditions for ecological regeneration.

The physical practices are documented.

The ecological outcomes should be measured separately.

For example:

- a planted native species is a **recorded intervention**;
- a GPS point is **provenance**;
- repeated survival data is a **measured outcome**;
- a biodiversity-restoration claim is an **interpretation** requiring a defined method.

## Story, implementation, evidence

The strongest Adelphi narrative keeps these three layers separate.

| Layer | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Story** | Two sisters with a family connection to farming want to return to the land |
| **Decision** | They purchase land in Gonzalo in 2022 |
| **Constraint** | They lack enough capital and infrastructure to operate the farm |
| **Intervention** | Public Nouns #69 helps fund critical infrastructure |
| **Implementation** | Production, poultry, nursery, biofactory, training, and data systems are built |
| **Evidence** | Farm records, measurements, MRV events, Hub records, and forecasts-vs-actuals |
| **Interpretation** | Economic, ecological, SDG, community, and replication conclusions |

> **The story explains why the work began. The evidence explains what happened after it began.**

## A note on the old “In their own words” passage

The previous version of this page presented the following project description as a direct quotation from the founders:

> “Adelphi is more than just an agricultural project...”

The repository does not provide a source that attributes those exact words directly to Yanny or Neury.

## How the story connects to Kokonut

Adelphi matters to Kokonut because it keeps the system anchored in a real operating context.

The story helps explain why the architecture now separates:

- local farm operators;
- capital governance;
- contribution coordination;
- Framework methodology;
- MRV;
- public data;
- analysis and automation.

No one of those layers is the farm.

The farm begins with people, land, and the work of operating it.

The shared infrastructure exists to make that work easier to finance, document, review, learn from, and eventually adapt elsewhere.
