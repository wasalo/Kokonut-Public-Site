# Adelphi's physical systems turn the farm model into something that can be inspected.

This page documents the **physical operating system** of Adelphi: crop beds, syntropic plots, perennial species, nursery, biofactory, poultry, soil inputs, training infrastructure, geospatial layout, and market-readiness work.

The purpose is not to infer impact from infrastructure alone.

It is to show:

1. what is physically documented;
2. what each component is designed to do;
3. which outputs are measured versus estimated;
4. what evidence would be needed to support stronger ecological, economic, or community claims.

## The farm system

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    LAND["15,725 m² mapped site"] --> AG["13,838 m² agricultural area"]

    AG --> CROPS["Short + medium + long-cycle crops"]
    AG --> SYNTROPIC["2 syntropic plots"]
    AG --> AGRO["Agroforestry + perennial species"]
    AG --> POULTRY["110 laying hens"]
    AG --> NURSERY["Nursery + biofactory"]
    AG --> GAZEBO["Training + community gazebo"]

    NURSERY --> INPUTS["Biochar + biological inputs"]
    POULTRY --> MANURE["Manure + nutrient cycling"]
    MANURE --> INPUTS
    INPUTS --> CROPS
    INPUTS --> SYNTROPIC

    CROPS --> RECORDS["Farm records"]
    SYNTROPIC --> RECORDS
    AGRO --> RECORDS
    POULTRY --> RECORDS
    NURSERY --> RECORDS
    GAZEBO --> RECORDS

    RECORDS --> MRV["MRV + review"]
    MRV --> HUB["Kokonut Hub + analysis"]
```

The system is designed so production, regeneration, inputs, animals, education, and evidence are connected.

The strength of any outcome claim still depends on the records generated by those components.

## Production across multiple time horizons

Adelphi does not depend on one crop cycle.

| Production horizon | Documented examples | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short cycle** | Lettuce, broccoli, spinach, tomatoes, arugula and other vegetables | Faster production cycles and near-term cash-flow potential |
| **Medium cycle** | Passion fruit, Indian yam and other seasonal crops | Seasonal diversification |
| **Long cycle** | Coconut and perennial agroforestry species | Long-duration production and ecological structure |
| **Continuous** | Poultry eggs | Recurring farm output and nutrient-cycle integration |

The mix supports diversification across time.

It does **not** guarantee diversified revenue.

Actual economic performance depends on:

- survival;
- harvest volume;
- losses;
- prices;
- labor;
- buyers;
- logistics;
- crop timing;
- poultry productivity.

## Biochar and soil-management practices

The repository documents biochar produced from bamboo found around the farm boundary.

The documented process uses pyrolysis and incorporates mineral material as part of the farm's soil-input practice.

The intended functions include:

- adding stable carbon-rich material to soil;
- supporting nutrient retention;
- supporting water-management objectives;
- creating habitat for soil biological processes;
- reusing locally available biomass.

Those are **mechanistic expectations**, not measured Adelphi outcomes by themselves.

A stronger Adelphi soil claim should include evidence such as:

| Evidence | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Application date and rate | Establishes what intervention actually occurred |
| Plot / location | Connects treatment to later observations |
| Baseline soil condition | Makes change interpretable |
| Repeated measurements | Shows direction over time |
| Moisture / EC / organic-matter context | Helps interpret soil response |
| Control or comparison where possible | Reduces attribution error |

[See the MRV evidence standard →](/ecosystem-wiki/kokonut-farms/measurement-reporting-and-verification)

## Syntropic plots and ground cover

Two plots are documented as dedicated syntropic implementations.

Their design uses multiple vertical and temporal layers.

| Layer | Intended function |
| --- | --- |
| **Ground cover** | Protect exposed soil and retain surface moisture |
| **Low layer** | Food production and species diversity |
| **Medium layer** | Fruit, vine, and intermediate biomass production |
| **Upper layer** | Longer-lived canopy, roots, biomass, habitat, and shade |

The farm also documents vegetative cover along terraced edges, including beard grass.

The intended purpose is to reduce bare soil and support erosion management.

A strong erosion-control claim still requires evidence such as:

- slope/location records;
- ground-cover percentage;
- rainfall period;
- erosion observations;
- before/after imagery;
- sediment or soil-loss indicators where practical.

## Poultry as production and nutrient infrastructure

Adelphi documents **110 free-range laying hens**.

The current production model estimates roughly **100 eggs per day** or **36,500 eggs per year**.

<Warning>
  The egg number is a **planning estimate** until reconciled against daily or periodic production records. Hen count and expected laying rate should not be presented as realized annual egg output.
</Warning>

The poultry system is designed to provide:

- eggs;
- manure;
- forage integration;
- organic matter;
- biological-input feedstock;
- an additional production stream.

The repository also documents Pangola grass as an on-site forage source.

That can support a reduced-external-feed strategy, but the actual degree of feed substitution should be measured through feed records rather than assumed.

### Documented nutrient loop

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    FORAGE["On-farm forage<br/>including Pangola grass"] --> HENS["110 hens"]
    HENS --> EGGS["Egg output"]
    HENS --> MANURE["Manure"]
    MANURE --> INPUTS["Biological inputs"]
    INPUTS --> BEDS["Crop + syntropic beds"]
    BEDS --> BIOMASS["Crop / forage biomass"]
    BIOMASS --> FORAGE
```

This is best described as a **designed nutrient loop**.

Its efficiency should be evaluated through feed, manure, input, soil, crop, and egg records.

## Organic certification and market readiness

The current repository documents Adelphi's **organic certification process as in progress through the Dominican Republic Ministry of Agriculture pathway**.

That status should be read literally:

**in progress ≠ certified.**

Potential distribution channels described in current planning include:

- specialized organic-food businesses;
- local and regional supermarkets;
- direct community sales.

These are **market-access goals**, not evidence of active buyer contracts or realized sales unless supported by current commercial records.

## Adjacent land is now a separate replication case

Older Adelphi documentation describes adjacent land as a future Adelphi expansion strategy.

The current Kokonut 101 and Regenerative Finance documentation now identifies roughly **50,000 m² of adjacent land under agreement as Kokonut Terra Viva**, a separate replication case entering **Phase I — Planning & Preparation**.

That distinction matters.

Adelphi is the first live reference farm.

Terra Viva is the next farm-scale test of whether Kokonut's shared Framework can move into a new implementation without simply expanding the same farm.

## How each system should become evidence

| Physical system | Strong current claim | Stronger claim requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crop beds** | Crops and production areas are documented | Harvest actuals, losses, quality, sales |
| **Syntropic plots** | Two syntropic plots are implemented | Repeated soil, vegetation, yield, and biodiversity evidence |
| **Nursery** | Nursery and species inventory exist | Propagation, survival, distribution, and conservation-status records |
| **Biochar** | On-site biochar practice is documented | Application records plus repeated soil measurements |
| **Poultry** | 110 hens are documented | Egg actuals, feed inputs, health, losses, manure records |
| **Biofactory** | Biological-input production is documented | Batch records, application rates, cost/use data, soil/crop response |
| **Gazebo** | Training/community space exists | Event records, attendance, learning and participation outcomes |
| **Certification** | Process is documented as in progress | Formal certification record |
| **Market channels** | Target channels are documented | Buyer agreements, sales, prices, distribution records |
| **Remote sensing / GPS** | Geospatial evidence can be collected | Repeated, reviewed records tied to specific claims |

### What the infrastructure does not prove by itself

- increased biodiversity;
- improved soil function;
- carbon sequestration;
- reduced erosion;
- lower input costs;
- higher yield;
- reliable market access;
- realized farm profitability;
- improved community learning;
- local food-security impact.

Those are outcomes to measure, not properties to infer automatically from design.
