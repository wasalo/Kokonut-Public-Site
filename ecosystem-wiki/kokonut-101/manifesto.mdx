# The people closest to the land should not be the people furthest from its decisions and value.

Kokonut begins with a simple conviction:

**Regenerative agriculture should strengthen the agency of the people caring for the land, not make them more dependent on distant institutions, opaque systems, or extractive intermediaries.**

Capital matters. Technology matters. Markets matter. Governance matters.

But none of them should become more important than the living systems, local knowledge, and people they are meant to support.

> **Kokonut exists to make regenerative agriculture easier to coordinate without making land, labor, data, or governance easier to extract.**

This manifesto is the value layer of the ecosystem. It does not replace governance, contracts, farm agreements, professional judgment, or local law.

It tells us what those systems should protect.

## The manifesto in one sentence

> **Build systems where regeneration increases local agency, useful work can earn recognition, important claims are inspectable, participation remains voluntary, and no single form of capital becomes sovereign over the whole ecosystem.**

That sentence is deliberately broader than "community-owned."

Ownership can mean different things:

- land title;
- governance rights;
- economic rights;
- contributor reputation;
- stewardship responsibility;
- access to shared infrastructure;
- participation in community benefit.

Kokonut should not collapse those rights into one vague promise.

The goal is to make each one **explicit, appropriately governed, and aligned with the people who create and maintain the value.**

---

## What we refuse

We refuse to use farmers, ecosystems, or communities as narrative decoration for financial speculation.

We refuse to treat capital contribution as the only form of value that deserves influence or recognition.

We refuse to describe forecasts as achieved outcomes.

We refuse to call something decentralized when one actor can still control the important decisions.

We refuse to make farm data public merely because technology allows it; privacy, consent, context, and legitimate confidentiality still matter.

We refuse to use blockchain where a simpler system serves people better.

We refuse to use AI as a substitute for farmers, agronomists, reviewers, community judgment, or accountable governance.

We refuse to make replication mean copying one farm into places with different ecology, culture, markets, operators, or laws.

We refuse to let "open" become an excuse for extracting local knowledge without attribution, consent, reciprocity, or benefit.

We refuse to design a system that can survive only while one founder, donor, grant, token cycle, or technology provider remains interested.

<Note>
  A system can be technically sophisticated and still violate the manifesto. Architecture is only aligned when it improves agency, accountability, evidence, and regenerative capacity in practice.
</Note>

## The Six Freedoms

The Six Freedoms describe what participation in Kokonut should protect.

They are aspirations implemented through different mechanisms—not a claim that every freedom is perfectly achieved today.

### 1. Freedom of Choice

**Participation should be voluntary, informed, and reversible where the system permits it.**

Capital-contributing Moloch members have a documented rage-quit mechanism that can protect exit rights under the applicable governance rules.

More broadly, Kokonut should avoid structures that trap participants through hidden dependencies, unclear rights, or deliberately high exit costs.

<Note>
  Freedom of Choice does not mean every commitment can be abandoned without consequence. Contracts, farm responsibilities, legal obligations, and approved work can create real duties. The principle is that those duties should be understandable before participation.
</Note>

### 2. Freedom of Work

**Useful contribution should have a path to visibility, standing, and recognition.**

Kokonut cannot be built only by people who contribute money.

Agronomy, field operations, research, software, governance, communications, finance, documentation, community coordination, and other useful work all create value.

The Guild model exists to make contribution legible without pretending every kind of work is interchangeable.

### 3. Freedom of Power

**No founder, token, Guild, agent, or technical system should become the unquestioned authority over the entire ecosystem.**

Capital governance has a defined domain.

Guilds have defined domains.

Farm operators have defined responsibilities.

Framework maintainers, MRV reviewers, contributors, and technical systems have their own scopes.

> **Power should be explicit enough to inspect and limited enough to challenge.**

### 4. Freedom of Access

**Participation should not depend only on pre-existing wealth.**

Kokonut currently separates at least two paths:

- capital participation through the Moloch DAO;
- contribution participation through useful work and Guild pathways.

Those paths can produce different rights.

Access does **not** mean every participant automatically receives land ownership, governance votes, economic rights, or tokens. Those distinctions should remain explicit.

[Read the governance layers →](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/dao-layers)

### 5. Freedom to Pay

**Shared resources should move through understandable, reviewable decisions.**

Farm funding, ecosystem budgets, bounties, partnerships, and other treasury-level commitments should use the governance process appropriate to them.

The principle is not "put every payment to a token vote."

The principle is:

- know who has authority;
- know what was approved;
- know what evidence or milestones apply;
- know where the funds moved;
- know how the decision can be reviewed.

### 6. Freedom of Opinion

**Dissent is part of coordination, not an attack on it.**

Participants should be able to question:

- proposals;
- budgets;
- forecasts;
- methods;
- impact claims;
- governance designs;
- technical architectures;
- leadership decisions;
- the manifesto itself.

Disagreement should be addressed through evidence, reasoning, governance, and respectful deliberation—not through status, intimidation, manufactured consensus, or suppression of criticism.

[Read the Governance Framework →](/ecosystem-wiki/the-kokonut-dao/governance-framework)

## Freedom requires responsibility

The Six Freedoms are not six ways to avoid accountability.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Choice requires informed consent" icon="door-open">
    People need enough information to understand what they are joining, contributing, funding, sharing, or agreeing to.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Work requires evidence" icon="clipboard-check">
    Contribution should be reviewable enough that recognition is tied to useful work rather than visibility, proximity, or self-promotion.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Power requires boundaries" icon="scale-balanced">
    Authority should have a defined scope, review path, and accountability mechanism.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Access requires clarity" icon="key">
    Different participation paths can carry different rights; those differences should be understandable rather than hidden.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Payment requires stewardship" icon="coins">
    Shared capital should be handled with budgets, milestones, records, and appropriate oversight.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Opinion requires good faith" icon="comments">
    Dissent should remain possible without turning disagreement into harassment, misinformation, or obstruction for its own sake.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Freedom without responsibility becomes another form of extraction.

## The Manifesto Test

Before a major governance change, partnership, funding mechanism, data practice, automation, or farm-level standard is adopted, Kokonut should be able to ask:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Does it increase or reduce local agency?" icon="people-group" iconType="regular">
    A system that becomes more efficient by making local operators less capable of acting, learning, or deciding may be optimizing the wrong thing.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Are the rights and responsibilities explicit?" icon="scale-balanced" iconType="regular">
    Land title, governance rights, economic rights, contribution reputation, stewardship duties, and community benefit should not be blurred together.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Can the important claims be inspected?" icon="magnifying-glass-chart" iconType="regular">
    Separate measurements from estimates, actuals from forecasts, attestations from scientific verification, and documented facts from aspirations.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Can affected participants challenge the decision?" icon="comments" iconType="regular">
    Governance should provide a meaningful path to question, review, revise, reject, or exit where appropriate.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Does it respect local context?" icon="map" iconType="regular">
    Do not import a farm design, governance process, data practice, or market assumption simply because it worked somewhere else.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Would it still be useful without the hype cycle?" icon="arrows-rotate" iconType="regular">
    Ask whether the decision still creates regenerative capacity if a grant ends, token attention disappears, AI becomes less fashionable, or one vendor is replaced.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Does it create dependency or reusable capacity?" icon="unlock" iconType="regular">
    Prefer systems that leave people with stronger knowledge, records, tools, infrastructure, and autonomy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  If a proposal improves one metric while materially weakening agency, consent, evidence, or regenerative capacity, the tradeoff should be stated openly rather than hidden behind the word "innovation."
</Note>

## Data, AI, and automation must answer to the same values

Kokonut's technical infrastructure will become more capable over time.

That makes constraints more important, not less.

### We commit to

- collecting data for clear purposes;
- respecting privacy, consent, and legitimate confidentiality;
- preserving provenance for material claims;
- distinguishing automated analysis from human review;
- avoiding manufactured consensus;
- keeping high-impact governance, financial, ecological, and agricultural decisions accountable to people;
- documenting assumptions and uncertainty;
- using AI to expand human capacity rather than erase human responsibility.

### We do not assume

- more data is always better;
- public data is always harmless;
- on-chain data is automatically true;
- a score is the same as judgment;
- an AI recommendation is the same as expertise;
- automation should remove every human checkpoint.

[Kokonut Intelligence](/kokonut-intelligence) and the [AI-agent architecture](/kokonut-x-ai-agents) should be evaluated by these same standards.

## Our commitments

### We commit to the land

Regenerative agriculture should leave living systems more capable of renewal.

That requires attention to soil, biodiversity, water, biomass, crop diversity, animals, climate conditions, and the long time horizons of perennial systems.

### We commit to local operators

Technology, governance, and funding should support the people responsible for the farm—not displace their knowledge or reduce them to execution labor for distant decision-makers.

### We commit to evidence

We will distinguish what is:

- documented;
- measured;
- self-reported;
- estimated;
- forecast;
- modeled;
- developing;
- aspirational.

Credibility should increase as evidence improves.

### We commit to plural governance

We will not treat one DAO, token, Guild, company, foundation, or technical system as the correct coordination mechanism for every responsibility.

### We commit to open infrastructure

Where appropriate, methods, documentation, schemas, and software should be inspectable and reusable.

The long-term test is whether useful Kokonut infrastructure can help communities become **less dependent** on the original builders.

### We commit to long horizons

Coconut trees and regenerative landscapes operate on timescales longer than a funding round, campaign, or software release.

The coordination systems around them should be designed with similar patience.

## The invitation

This manifesto is for people who believe:

**regeneration should increase agency, not dependency;**

**capital should be accountable to the systems it enters;**

**useful work should have a visible path to recognition;**

**local knowledge should remain powerful even when technology becomes more sophisticated;**

**important claims should become easier to inspect;**

**open infrastructure should make the next community more capable, not more dependent;**

**and disagreement should make the system more rigorous rather than less welcoming.**

Kokonut does not need everyone to agree on every implementation detail.

It needs contributors willing to protect these constraints while testing better ways to coordinate.
