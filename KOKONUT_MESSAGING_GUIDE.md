**Status:** Editorial baseline for site-wide narrative alignment  
**Version:** 1.0 · November 2026

**Purpose:** Keep the **What / Why / How / Proof / Participate** story understandable and consistent throughout the public site. This is an editorial guide, **not a substitute for contracts, canonical farm data, current deployments, or technical PRDs**.

## 1. One-sentence definition

> **Kokonut Network is an open coordination infrastructure for regenerative agriculture.**

Plain-language alternative for first-time visitors:

> **Kokonut is an open network helping communities build regenerative farms.**

Neither formulation means Kokonut centrally owns every farm or that participation automatically provides land, equity, treasury, or investment rights.

### Short description (under 45 words)

Kokonut helps communities build regenerative farms, coordinate the people and resources around them, measure what happens, and turn lessons into reusable infrastructure for future farms. It starts with locally operated agriculture and adds shared methods, appropriate governance, evidence, and open technology.

### Medium description (websites, partners, talks)

Kokonut Network connects locally operated regenerative farms with people, capital coordination, shared agricultural methods, governance, evidence, and open technology. Starting with Kokonut Adelphi in the Dominican Republic, the goal is to make each farm easier to plan, support, measure, improve, and learn from—while keeping local operations and protected decisions with the appropriate people and organizations.

### Mission

Help build productive regenerative systems by connecting local farms, communities, available resources, shared knowledge, governance, and evidence-aware technology.

### Vision

A distributed network of locally operated regenerative farms that share infrastructure, learn from one another, create diverse forms of value, and reinvest available capacity into stronger landscapes and communities.

### Positioning line

**Building open coordination infrastructure for regenerative agriculture—from the ground up.**

### Public headline

**Grow the future. Regenerate the present.**

Preserve it as the homepage headline; use the clear definition and mission statement immediately below.

## 3. The reusable regeneration cycle

**Grow → Measure → Verify → Govern → Learn → Regenerate**

This is a communication model, **not a universal chronological transaction pipeline**.

1. **Grow:** Real farm operations—land, soil, water, trees, food crops, people, and productive infrastructure.
2. **Measure:** Context-rich observations, financial records, production data, and ecological evidence.
3. **Verify:** Qualified review of sources, methods, limitations and claimed outcomes.
4. **Govern:** Relevant farm operators, entity signers, DAO governance and contributors apply distinct decision rights.
5. **Learn:** Kokonut Framework, Kokonut Intelligence, and local teams use reviewed results to improve methods.
6. **Regenerate:** Where actual resources, rights and approvals permit, reinvest in productive capacity, community well-being and the next improvement cycle.

**Important nuance:** Measurement is not verification. An onchain attestation is not scientific certification. An AI report is not independent review.

### The economic flywheel

Available capital and coordinated support → approved productive farm work → actual food, products, services and documented outcomes → economic activity and reviewed evidence → lawful and authorized reinvestment → stronger farm and community capacity.

Do **not** compress that chain to “token appreciation → farm profits.” Token valuations, restricted capital, invoices, crop forecasts, revenue and distributable free surplus represent **different financial states**.

## 4. What makes Kokonut different

- **Farm first, not token first.** Start with productive agricultural needs and evidence.
- **Network, not plantation.** Different farms retain appropriate local operation and variation; share methodologies, tools, knowledge, and standards.
- **Different decisions, different authorities.** DAO, Guild, farm operator, legal company, research reviewer and software agent roles are not interchangeable.
- **Evidence before impact claims.** Data provenance and review precede strong public ecological or financial conclusions.
- **Technology is a means.** Blockchain and AI help coordinate, inspect, and learn. Neither by itself demonstrates regenerative outcomes.
- **More than financial capital.** The Framework also considers Natural, Social, Human, Material, Intellectual, Cultural, and Health forms.

## 5. The Eight Forms of Capital

Use the Framework vocabulary exactly: **Natural, Financial, Social, Human, Material, Intellectual, Cultural, Health**.

When explaining to the general public, give relatable examples of *possible* outcomes (soil health, jobs, farming knowledge, equipment, community networks), **not** an implication that all eight have been measured or improved at every site.

The six Pillars of Value can be phrased as questions:
1. What is changing?
2. Who is affected?
3. How much changes?
4. What did the intervention contribute?
5. What could fail?
6. What public goods are created?

Deeper definitions, boundaries and measurement rules belong in the [Kokonut Framework](/kokonut-framework/Introduction).

## 7. Terminology: use and avoid

| Prefer | Avoid / qualify | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Locally operated / local stewardship | “Community-owned land” without the legal instrument | Ownership and participation are distinct |
| Documented reference value | Live metric, if no live telemetry exists | Don't turn snapshots into current state |
| Reviewed evidence / reported observation | Verified impact without method and reviewer | Evidence maturity varies |
| Planned or proposed service | Live service based only on a project listing | Release gates differ |
| Restricted / unlocked / reconciled capital | “Raised cash available for farm spending” | Accounting and rights are distinct |
| Human-supervised agent | Autonomous certifier or unrestricted treasury controller | Permission and review boundary |
| Public project page / launch listing | Verified token configuration or trading revenues | Listing does not prove those details |
| Future financial research | A promised yield, dividend, buyback or farmland claim | Financial instruments need separate approval |

## 8. Evidence and maturity guardrails

For each operational or financial claim, explicitly identify:
- **Owner/source:** relevant farm, organization, source repository or onchain record.
- **State:** observed, documented, forecast, reviewed, independently verified, proposed, deployed, or inactive.
- **As-of date:** when a changing figure was recorded.
- **Limitations:** uncertainty, missing data, unverified assumptions, transferability and rights.
- **Decision authority:** who can authorize spending, publishing and governance actions.

**Adelphi:** The farm is the first live reference; public evidence is reachable from [the Data Hub](https://hub.kokonut.network/projects/41). Its page and Hub are sources for dated farm details; don't treat snapshot harvest forecasts as settled sales.

**Syntropic Agent:** Its publicly announced Virtuals and Robinhood/PONS launch destinations are distinct from ACP services, NEAR orchestration, MetaLeX securities infrastructure, creator-fee receipts and approved farm spending. Verify each independently from current owning records.

**Finance:** Farm cashflow, token trading activity, restricted balances and token market capitalization are not interchangeable. Financial rights arise from enforceable agreements and authorized corporate/governance acts—not merely because a token or Revenue Container exists.
